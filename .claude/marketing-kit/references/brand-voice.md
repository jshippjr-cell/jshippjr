# Brand Voice & Marketing Preferences — Chordential

> **THE BRIEF WINS. THIS FILE IS ITS DEPUTY, NOT ITS REPLACEMENT.**
>
> The authority is `docs/marketing/00-brief.md` in this repository — ratified
> canon, amended by the founder, and the thing every marketing session reads
> first. **Read it before you produce anything.** What follows is the subset the
> twelve installed skills need inline, because a skill reads this file by
> construction and might never follow a pointer.

> Where this file and the brief differ, **the brief is right and this file is

## Priority Task
Thirty named agency producers in the US Southeast, reached by hand, toward one
founding engagement — and the composers who would write the music. **Not a
demand engine**: it is stood down for sixty days (brief §0.5). Content exists so
a stranger who gets a message finds a real person; it is not the engine.

## Content Types
**The founder does not want auto-generated social content** (2026-09-11): three
drafts failed and he stopped the practice. A skill that writes posts for him is
being used against a ruling. What is wanted instead: research, briefs, structure,
and editing of words he said. He talks, the machine shapes.

## Target Audience
Agency producers and heads of production who commission original music for
campaigns — then executive producers at production companies, then business
affairs. Separately and in a different voice: **composers and engineers**, where
the rules differ (see Banned Words).

## Brand Voice & Tone
First person, as the person doing the work. Plain, warm, unpolished. Lumpy
sentences beat balanced ones. No aphorisms, no three-beat rhythm, no line shaped
to be quoted. Honest over certain — *"not yet observed"* is a complete sentence.
Never rank ourselves against a named peer, and never against an adjacent craft.

The founder is **Jon Shipp**, Miami. Not a composer: he has ideas most days, used
to compose, stopped, and says he lacks the discipline to finish them. He admires
the tenacity and focus he does not have, and built this to help the people who do
have it get their work made and get paid for it. He is also AV director at Encore.
**Write from the chair he actually sits in.** Four early drafts gave vendor advice
about delivering music, which he has never done commercially, and every one read
as false.

## Default Output Format
Whatever the skill produces, in his voice, with every claim checkable. Deliverables
belong in `docs/marketing/deliverables/<role>.md` and nowhere else.

## Banned Words & Phrases
**Never, to a buyer:** platform · dashboard · portal · workspace · OS · software ·
tool · app · AI-powered · intelligent · automated · seamless · end-to-end ·
hassle-free · one-stop · full-service · we handle everything · vendor · talent /
resources / freelancers · world-class · award-winning · trusted by · guaranteed ·
litigation-proof · bulletproof · cheap / affordable · royalty-free / stock /
library **as our offer** · full buyout · work-made-for-hire · procurement-grade ·
workflow · partner / creative partner · clearance-certified as an adjective ·
department / team of / offices / studios (plural) / our team · on time / fast
turnaround · *the music is the easy part* · *the music department you don't have
to build* · hub.

**AI as a subject**, outside the single sentence explaining why human authorship
is a legal fact. Never as a badge.

**Four rules that are not word lists**, each one the founder striking his own copy:

1. **Never define a word to the reader.** *"Composed means…"* puts the writer
   above the reader. Say what we do *for them*: *we write it for your campaign*.
2. **Never volunteer a weakness.** *"No paying client yet"* is off every surface.
   Claiming a client we do not have remains impossible, and every demonstration is
   still labelled one — that label is not an admission.
3. **Never flatter the reader.** *"I learn more from producers than from anyone"*
   is false and audible. An ask stands on its own.
4. **Never lift ourselves by lowering an adjacent craft.** Film, games and
   television are where our composers come from.

**Money.** Never to a buyer: no budget, fee, price or rate in any public content.
The price lives on one surface, the proposal, after a call. **Lifted for a
creator**: what a composer is paid, keeps, and when it arrives is published in
full, stated as terms and never as generosity, never comparative.

## Formatting Constraints
Three posts a week maximum and never a fourth — and see Content Types: the founder
has stood posting down. No engagement bait. No link to ourselves in a comment, at
any contribution count. A community gets ten genuinely useful contributions before
anything else.

## Do / Don't Examples
**Do:** *"I have a folder full of unfinished things."* · *"There are no case
studies yet."* · *"We clear it so your business affairs team can sign without a
cover note."*
**Don't:** *"Great campaigns deserve more than great music."* · *"Just press
publish."* · *"Music rarely fails creatively. It fails operationally."* — all three
are real lines that were cut for being written rather than said.

## Sonic Identity
**No machine-made music, anywhere, including as a demonstration.** This is the one
refusal that would contradict the whole position, and it is absolute. If a skill
offers to generate audio, music or a soundtrack, **decline and say why.** The only
recording on the site is the founder's own, there because a music house should not
play machine-made music on its own site.

## Visual Identity
Invented brands only — AURORA, Vance Athletic, HALVARD — labelled a demonstration
wherever they appear. No real trademark, no client logo, no fabricated receipt,
testimonial or case study. Name a real organisation only when confident it exists;
mark anything unconfirmed **(verify)**. Never name a private individual from memory.
