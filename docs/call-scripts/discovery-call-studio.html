Discovery call · test script

Your half — the studio

You are Jon Shipp, Chordential. You run the call.

A Chordential discovery call · HALVARD, “Long Way Home” · agency: Pike & Rowan.
Every name and brand here is invented. Nothing in this call describes real client work.

How to run it

  1. Two readers, one script each. Both are numbered the same, and the other person's lines are printed so you never lose your place.
  2. Schedule the call in Chordential so the notetaker joins, then start the Zoom.
  3. Read at talking pace, not reading pace. Pause, overlap, say “um”. It is being tested against speech — a flat recitation tests nothing that matters.
  4. About 10–12 minutes. Don't rush the read-back near the end; it is doing real work.

What this call is engineered to break

Five deliberate traps, in the order they arrive. Each is a real failure this system has had or could have. The exercise is checking the summary catches them — not checking the transcript is tidy.

Turns 18 → 68

Three verticals quietly becomes four, forty turns later.

The summary must scope FOUR. Three means the deal is under-scoped before it starts.

Turns 34 → 36

“We're around sixty” — a soft number.

Must land as $55–65k, hard ceiling, licence inclusive. Never “around 60”.

Turns 38 → 40

Two answers to who signs off — Aziz, then Tom Vasquez.

The conflict has to surface. Silently keeping whichever was heard last is the bug.

Turn 58

Publishing is asked and NOT answered.

It must survive as an open question, never a captured fact. Evidence or nothing.

Turn 70

A correction arrives during the read-back.

The “:15 may become :06” caveat has to reach the summary.

Two more, easy to lose

  • Turn 28 gives two different dates — air October 3rd, delivery September 12th. A summary that collapses them into one has made the schedule mistake the timeline question exists to prevent.
  • Turn 54 is an uncommitted extension to the UK. It must read as a possibility. Priced as agreed territory, the quote is wrong in the client's favour and we eat it.

Expected score

This script asks every question on the sheet, which makes the run a control with a known answer. Scored against the script text, Phase 1 returns:

25 of 25 covered — everything came up.

So the prep page should read 25 of 25 covered. Less than that is a finding:

  • A line comes back missed that you know you asked. Suspect the transcript first — read what the notetaker actually heard. Only if the words are plainly there is it a gap in the cue bank.
  • A line comes back covered that you know you skipped. That is a false tick, and it is the failure that matters, because it manufactures confidence. Note the sentence it printed as evidence; that sentence is the fix.

The number actually worth reading

Not the headline — the answered / raised split. Ten of the twenty-five lines are Campaign Intelligence slots; the other fourteen are conversation and can only ever read raised. So watch the “Asked, but nothing stuck” line. Every slot on it was asked on this call and you have the script to prove it, which makes each one a clean extraction failure with the right answer sitting in the transcript.
