Discovery call · test script

Your half — the client

You are Marisa Del Rio, senior producer at Pike & Rowan.

A Chordential discovery call · HALVARD, “Long Way Home” · agency: Pike & Rowan.
Every name and brand here is invented. Nothing in this call describes real client work.

How to run it

  1. Two readers, one script each. Both are numbered the same, and the other person's lines are printed so you never lose your place.
  2. Schedule the call in Chordential so the notetaker joins, then start the Zoom.
  3. Read at talking pace, not reading pace. Pause, overlap, say “um”. It is being tested against speech — a flat recitation tests nothing that matters.
  4. About 10–12 minutes. Don't rush the read-back near the end; it is doing real work.

Staying in character

  • You are the client, not the tester. Don't help. If Jon doesn't ask something, don't volunteer it — half of what this measures is what a real call loses.
  • Two of your lines are corrections, and they matter. At turn 40 you correct who signs off; at turn 70 you correct the cutdown length during the read-back. Deliver both as genuine afterthoughts, not as cues.
  • At turn 58 you don't know the answer. Say so plainly and move on. A question that gets no answer has to survive the call as an open question.
  • Improvise at the edges — an “um”, a false start, a different word — as long as the facts land. Speech that sounds like speech is the point.
