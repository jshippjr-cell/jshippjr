Cross-call debrief

What repeated in every room

1 · Honesty was the top-scoring move in all five calls — by a wide margin

Every credibility breakthrough traces to a concession, not a claim: admitting the no-AI guarantee can't audit laptops (Dana: led to her most-engaged moment), "we're early, no logo wall" (Elena: directly earned the sampler invitation), "on one listen, maybe your CMO can't hear the difference" (Priya), volunteering the indemnity carve-outs unprompted (Marcus: "the first non-slippery answer I've gotten in three weeks"), and "I'm not promising final masters Thursday" (Sam). The pattern: concede the true limitation before the buyer finds it, then differentiate inside what remains.

2 · One differentiator landed in all five rooms: provable human authorship

The AI-provenance attestation / session-file archive was the only claim no buyer could shrug off. Dana: "the first thing you've said my music houses genuinely don't do." Elena: "the only thing my bench doesn't have — lead with that next time, not the tidy folders." Sam: "quietly the most valuable page in the binder." Marcus and Priya both routed it to legal/CMO. Lead with the attestation; let "tidier paperwork" be the supporting cast. Clearance alone is NOT differentiating against good music houses — Elena proved a knowledgeable supervisor will call that bluff.

3 · Techniques get caught; structure gets signed

Both times the rep used a recognizable sales move, the buyer named it out loud ("you're doing a technique on me right now"; "that's the second trial-close"). But every structured concession closed: the signed brief as revision referee, the kill fee capped-with-conditions, the free re-direction put in writing. Buyers in this market are professional negotiators — trade terms, don't deploy moves.

4 · The playbook's mechanics held under fire

  • Talk ratio: 43–45% rep across all five calls, including the 12-minute one.
  • Questions: 13–14 in full calls; correctly compressed to 7 under deadline pressure without skipping scope confirmation.
  • Price in the back third, precise, three tiers: executed in every call; recommending the floor tier twice produced the strongest trust moments ("that's either honest or a very good trick").
  • One teach moment: the calls that converted each had exactly one, and it was the turn the deal advanced on (music-system reframe for Marcus; master/publishing rent-trap for Priya; provenance-warranty trend for Dana).
  • LAER (pause → label → clarify → respond): defused every hard objection; the two interruptions the rep suffered both followed answers that ran past ~45 seconds.

5 · Coaching flags that recurred

  • Monologue drift after a good answer — twice interrupted. The fix both times: "short version" restatement in one sentence.
  • Detail slips under pressure (":60" vs ":30", 120 vs 140 locations, garbled deposit terms) — each recovered by owning it instantly, but each cost a beat of exactly the reliability being stress-tested.
  • Language sensitivity with burned buyers: "when the lawsuit hit" nearly detonated the Marcus call — it was a demand letter. With legally anxious buyers, precision of language IS the product demo.
  • Overreach on access: asking for client business affairs before clearing the gatekeeper (Dana: hard no), pushing for a meeting with no live brief (Elena: "that's how vendors talk themselves off my list"). The recovery pattern that worked: name the overreach, shrink the ask.

The sentence to write on the wall

From the supervisor who's watched vendors try for fourteen years: "Everyone auditions with their best work; almost nobody auditions with their reliability. The vendor who makes my worst Tuesday easier is the vendor who gets the anthem brief eventually."

Open items the simulations exposed (real work, not roleplay)

  • The spec-demo gap: two buyers expect three demos in 48 hours on spec; Chordential's no-spec position needs a productized answer (the matched sampler worked once — formalize it) before the September-style competitive searches.
  • Deposit vs. net-90 collision: the 40%-deposit / net-90-balance structure worked in simulation; it should be written into standard terms before a real procurement team tests it.
  • Indemnity language: two calls advanced specifically on "send the verbatim clause." That document must exist and be counsel-reviewed before it's promised again.
  • Kill-fee and re-direction policies: both were invented under pressure. Codify: kill fee = deposit, conditioned on verified campaign death; first-engagement V1 direction-miss = free re-direction.
