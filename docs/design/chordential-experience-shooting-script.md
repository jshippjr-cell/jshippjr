# THE CHORDENTIAL EXPERIENCE — SHOOTING SCRIPT
### A five-minute short film navigated with a mouse
### Working title: **"THE ONE THAT SHIPS"**
*Supersedes the storyboard sections of the Experience Bible (v1). The lighting,
tempo (96 BPM), physics, sound policy, and ship-gate checklist from v1 remain law;
this document replaces the STORY. Two chairs wrote it: the CREATIVE DIRECTOR
(story + camera) and the MOTION DESIGNER (physics + translation). Every scene
carries both voices. The proof is the spec.*

---

## THE PICTURE

**Protagonist:** YOU — an Executive Producer with another impossible campaign to
get out the door. The site is shot in second person. Chordential is not the hero;
Chordential is the room the hero finally walks into.

**Logline:** An EP drowning in revisions, rights problems, and deadline pressure
walks into a control room where the details survive production — and leaves
confident enough to press the button.

**The tension:** Scene 1 is the protagonist's real week — chaos. The film earns
its calm. Nothing beautiful is allowed on screen until the pressure has been
*felt.* Every problem raised in Scene 1 is paid off, on camera, before the end.

**The early reveal (the 15-second rule):** By the end of Scene 3 — inside the
first 15 seconds of scroll — the visitor must think: *"Wait. This isn't a music
studio. It's an operating system for campaign music."* We hand them the phone;
we do not describe the aluminum.

**The signature (the one interaction):** THE STEM EXPLOSION. One click:
master → stems → deliverables → versions → organization → reassemble. It teaches
the entire product in five seconds. The homepage is built around it; everything
before it builds to it; everything after it refers back to it.

**Camera grammar (applies to every scene):**
- Default lens **35mm equivalent** — intimate, slight presence. Detail inserts on
  **85mm** — compressed, shallow. (Translation: two perspective/parallax presets;
  "35" = deeper parallax separation + wider FOV transforms, "85" = flattened
  planes, tighter DOF blur on fore/background.)
- Movements: **push** (scale + parallax convergence on scroll), **dolly** (lateral
  plane slide), **rack focus** (blur handoff between planes), **orbit** (3D
  rotation around a held object), **settle** (spring landing at rest).
- **Never cut inside a scene.** One continuous camera per room. Between rooms:
  **match cuts** only — an object in the outgoing frame becomes an object in the
  incoming frame (light → light, wave → wave, dot → dot).
- Every scene is staged on **three object planes** — foreground / midground /
  background — that move independently. No flat scenes.
- **Handheld drift** (±2px, 0.1Hz noise) is reserved for tension; the camera
  stabilizes when Chordential enters. Stability = the brand entering the story.

**Anticipation law:** every room ends with a *pull* — light under the next door,
an unresolved tone, a question on screen — so the visitor scrolls to find out,
never to see "what else they offer."

## COLD OPEN — SCENE 1: "YOUR WEEK" (the chaos)

**STORY.** No brand. No beauty. We open inside the protagonist's pressure.

**CAMERA.**
Fade from black — one full second of true dark.
85mm, tight, HANDHELD. The frame never quite rests.
Fragments surface in shallow focus and die before you finish reading them —
the texture of a bad week:

> *"client wants a different track — by Friday"* ·
> *"the sync license fell through"* · *"v9_FINAL_final2.wav"* ·
> *"legal flagged the sample"* · *"can we get stems? today?"*

Type jitters half a pixel. Notification pings render as harsh little light-stabs
(visual even with sound off). The color is wrong on purpose — the warmth is
missing; ink without ember. Pressure builds on the 96 BPM grid but *syncopated* —
off-beat arrivals, the only scene allowed to break time.

Then the noise thins. Beat of black. One line, steady, dead center, lit plainly:

> **Campaigns don't fail because of creativity.**
> **They fail because the details don't survive production.**

Hold two bars. The visitor thinks: *this is about me.*

**MOTION NOTES (MD).** Fragments: opacity/blur-in at 85mm preset, lifetime ≤ 1.2s,
staggered off-grid (±90ms), max 3 on screen. Handheld = Perlin ±2px on the scene
container. Light-stabs: 60ms white-warm flashes at 4% opacity, radial. The thesis
line arrives ON the beat — the first on-grid event of the film (order emerging).
Reduced-motion: fragments as a still collage, thesis line lit, no jitter.

**PULL:** under the last line, at the bottom edge of the dark — a thin strip of
warm light, like a door not quite closed. Scroll approaches it.

## SCENE 2: "THE DOOR" (someone's already here)

**STORY.** Relief begins — but earned, quiet, almost eerie. Someone turned the
console on before you arrived.

**CAMERA.**
MATCH CUT — the door-light strip widens into the frame; we're through it.
35mm. The handheld drift **settles to a slow, confident push** — the exact moment
Chordential enters the story is the moment the camera stops shaking.
Silence rendered visually: the air stills, grain softens.
Then, midground: **a red record light comes on.** One small click of presence.
Focus pulls to it. Hold one bar.
Wordmark emerges from the shadow beside it — not animated in, *found* by the
room's warm key as the push continues. The logo breathes, once per two bars.

Line, small, beneath: **"Come in. It's quieter in here."**

**MOTION NOTES.** The settle is a spring on the container's noise amplitude
(2px → 0 over one bar) — cheap, enormous. Record light: 6px ember dot, bloom
earned, breath at half-tempo. Foreground plane: a music stand silhouette slides
2% against the push (35mm separation). Nav appears ONLY now, top-right, arpeggio
stagger — per the v1 rule that nothing precedes the room.

**PULL:** the record light's glow spills onto something below the fold — the edge
of a screen. What screen?

## SCENE 3: "THE MACHINE" (the reveal — inside 15 seconds)

**STORY.** The turn. This room isn't a vibe — it's an instrument. The visitor
realizes what Chordential actually is *before* any craft romance.

**CAMERA.**
Continuous push arrives at the console — RACK FOCUS from the record light
(foreground, going soft) to what the glow was spilling on: **a live surface.**
Not faders. Panels. Campaign Intelligence filling itself in. A pipeline arc with
one node breathing. A readiness ledger ticking 7/9 → 8/9. Version names
assembling themselves: `AURORA_Anthem_60_MASTER_v3_FINAL`.
The machine is *already working* — nobody is touching it.

One line over the surface:

> **This isn't a music studio.**
> **It's an operating system for campaign music.**

Sub-line, smaller: *composition, clearance, versions, delivery — run like an
engineering discipline.*

**CAMERA (cont.).** Slow dolly LEFT along the surface — three panels pass:
*Intelligence* (facts arriving with provenance stamps) → *Clearance* (a
certificate row flipping to CLEARED) → *Delivery* (eleven file glyphs
assembling). Never cut. The dolly ends on a single object sitting apart from the
console, lit like a museum piece: **a master tape.** Focus racks to it. Hold.

**MOTION NOTES.** The panels are real UI drawn in the film's grade (dark, warm,
rim-lit) — honest screens of the actual product surfaces, alive on the same
event grammar as ChordOS (arrive/halo/tick). Dolly = translateX on midground
plane, fore/background at 1.4× / 0.6×. The ledger tick uses the true count-up
primitive. 60fps: panels are pre-composited layers, one canvas for the arc.

**PULL:** the master tape. A caption fades up beneath it: **"Touch it."**

## SCENE 4: "THE STEM EXPLOSION" (the signature — the whole site in one gesture)

**STORY.** The visitor performs the product themselves. This is the scene the
site exists for; everything before it was the setup.

**CAMERA.**
The master hangs center-frame in warm air, reflections beneath it. 35mm, ORBIT
armed: pointer movement rotates the piece ±10°, light tracking across its face.
Its waveform breathes faintly — it is *playing* somewhere in another room.

ON CLICK (or scroll-through on touch):
**The master bursts — in tempo — into five stems.**
Drums / bass / strings / synths / voice: five strips fan out in 3D on spring
physics, each with its own living waveform, each labeled in the spec-mono voice.
Camera pulls back 10% to hold the constellation.

Beat. Then the stems **cascade into what they become**:
each stem trails into its deliverables — :60 / :30 / :15 / :06 / 9:16 glyphs —
which stack into the versions ladder (`v1 → v2 → v3 FINAL`), which snaps into a
tidy manifest with a cue sheet and a wax **CLEARANCE CERTIFIED** seal stamping
down with real weight (85mm insert, one bar).

Hold one bar. Then everything **reassembles in reverse** — deliverables fold into
stems, stems into the master — spring-settled, whole again.

Line: **"One master. Every stem. Every cutdown. Every clearance. Delivered."**

Hover any stem while exploded: it SOLOS — its waveform brightens and animates
alone, the others dim to 55% (the product's own sibling-dim grammar). Audio-on
visitors hear the actual solo.

**MOTION NOTES.** Explosion: 5 bodies, staggered 1/16ths, overshoot 4%, damping
.8. Cascade: each transformation is a match-move (stem strip morphs to glyph
row — scale/position tween, no crossfade). The stamp: scale 1.5 → 1.0 with a
2-frame hold at contact + 4% frame shake, ONE time. Reassembly = reversed
choreography at 60% duration. All transform/opacity; waveforms on one shared
canvas; DPR capped 2. Touch: scroll scrubs the sequence (pinned, 2 viewport
heights); tap solos.

**PULL:** as the master settles, five of the Scene-1 fragments flicker faintly
around it — the bad week, remembering itself. Scroll.

## SCENE 5: "THE PRESSURE, PAID OFF" (tension resolves on camera)

**STORY.** Every Scene-1 problem returns — and the machine answers each one,
on screen, in order. Callbacks, not claims. The knot untied where we tied it.

**CAMERA.**
35mm, slow continuous push through five stations — one per problem. Each
station: the ORIGINAL chaos fragment (same type, same jitter — a flash of the
cold open) drifts in… then the camera racks focus past it to the answer sitting
calm and lit behind it, and the fragment **dissolves into the answer's light.**

> *"the sync license fell through"* → the clearance certificate, sealed:
> **Original. Cleared worldwide. Nothing to fall through.**
> *"v9_FINAL_final2.wav"* → the version ladder: **v1 · v2 · v3 FINAL. Named by a
> system, not a panic.**
> *"can we get stems? today?"* → the manifest: **Stems ship WITH the master.**
> *"legal flagged the sample"* → **Sample-free, attested — certificate on file.**
> *"by Friday"* → the readiness ledger ticking to **9/9 · release ready.**

Each dissolve lands ON the beat — chaos was off-grid; resolution is in time.

**MOTION NOTES.** Fragment→answer: rack focus = blur handoff (fragment 0→6px,
answer 6px→0) + luminance shift; dissolve is 60% duration ease-in. One station
in view at a time (scroll-driven). The heartbeat of this scene: tension =
syncopation, relief = the grid. Reduced-motion: five still pairings,
strikethrough on the problem.

**PULL:** the last station's light dims to a single breathing ember below —
the record light again. We've seen it before. It means something now.

---

## SCENE 6: "THE ONE THAT SHIPS" (confidence — the invitation)

**STORY.** The room at rest. The protagonist — calmer than they arrived — is
offered the chair.

**CAMERA.**
The push ends. Wide, still frame: the console asleep but warm, the record light
breathing, the master tape whole on its stand. For the first time in the film,
**nothing needs anything from you.**
Type, center, lit by the room:

> **Your campaign, scored.**
> **Composed. Cleared. Delivered. The one that ships.**

The CTA fades up on the bar — **"Begin a session"** — magnetic, physical,
console-button press. Honest sub-line: *"Tell us about the project. We reply
with an approach and a price range."*
The footer is one quiet program line. The final idle: the record light,
breathing, forever. The session never quite ends.

**MOTION NOTES.** This scene's motion budget: the breath, the magnetic CTA, and
the lantern. Nothing else moves. Stillness is the payoff and must be protected
from future "improvements."

## PRODUCTION LAWS (inherited from Bible v1 — unchanged and binding)
96 BPM grid · one room key light · lantern cursor · physics with mass · never
autoplay, D-major sound identity, visual twin for every audio moment · 60fps on
mid-tier hardware · reduced-motion = gorgeous stills · keyboard-complete ·
honesty (demo brands labeled, no fake logos) · the ship-gate checklist per scene
· **the proof is the spec.**

## BUILD ORDER (after approval)

spine) → S3 reveal → S5 payoffs → S6 close → sound layer → checklist pass per
scene, in the can only at script fidelity.
