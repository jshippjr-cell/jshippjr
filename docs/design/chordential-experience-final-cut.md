# THE CHORDENTIAL EXPERIENCE — FINAL CUT
### "THE ONE THAT SHIPS" · commercial recut
*Supersedes the story order, pacing, and ALL copy of the shooting script. Camera
grammar, motion physics, 96 BPM grid, lighting, sound policy, and the ship gate
carry over unchanged by reference. Three chairs signed this cut: the Creative

---

## The corrections this cut makes

1. **The story ran backwards.** It opened on our room and worked toward the
   client. Recut: **Campaign → Pressure → Evidence → System → Confidence →
   Invitation.** The studio is no longer a scene. The studio is never the hero —
   it survives only as one prop (the record light: a state, not a place).
2. **The opening was 20 seconds of cinema. It is now 6 seconds to reassurance.**
   Modern attention budget: the visitor must feel *understood* by second 6 and
   see *evidence* by the first scroll.
3. **The target emotion changes from "impressed" to** ***"finally — someone gets
   it."*** Impressing is for awards juries. Reassuring is for people with a
   campaign due Friday. Everything cinematic now works in service of calm.
4. **The copy is rewritten in the Reassurance Voice** (§ below). The screen may
   be cinematic; the sentences never are.

---

## THE REASSURANCE VOICE (binding copy law)

Written like a calm senior producer on the phone with a stressed one.

- Short declaratives. Almost no adjectives. Zero metaphors in on-screen copy —
  the poetry lives in light and motion; the words stay plain.
- **"You" carries the site.** "We" appears only to take responsibility.
- Every claim is concrete and checkable (stems, cutdowns, clearance, named
  versions, price range) — nothing aspirational, nothing "world-class."
- Never announce feelings ("imagine the confidence…"). Produce them.
- Test for every line: *would a tired EP nod, or roll their eyes?*

## THE FILM — six beats

### BEAT 1 · "FRIDAY" — 0:00–0:03
**STORY.** Open already inside their problem. No logo first, no black-hold.
**CAMERA.** 0.5s fade from dark. 85mm, the faintest handheld. Three pressure
fragments are ALREADY on screen as shallow-focus texture — *"new track by
Friday" · "license fell through" · "v9_FINAL_final2"* — while the thesis lands
center by second 2, in time:

> **Campaigns don't fail on creativity.**
> **They fail when the details don't survive production.**

**MOTION.** Fragments pre-seeded (no waiting for them), thesis on the beat.
Total elapsed: 3 seconds.

### BEAT 2 · "SOMEONE GETS IT" — 0:03–0:07
**STORY.** The reassurance, immediately. The camera settles (the one inherited
form-move: stability = us arriving). The record light clicks on beside the
wordmark — we're on, we're here. Copy, plain:

> **You need the track, the stems, the cutdowns, and the clearance — and you
> needed them Friday.**
> **That's the part we run.**

Nav appears. Sub-line: *Chordential — original music for campaigns, produced on
an operating system we built for exactly this.* (The product is now named at
second ~6, not second 20.)
**PULL:** below the fold, lit: a master tape. Caption: **"This is a delivery.
Touch it."**

### BEAT 3 · "EVIDENCE" — the signature, first scroll
**STORY.** Proof before promises. The stem explosion, exactly as scripted
(master → five living stems → cutdowns → version ladder → cue sheet + clearance
seal stamping → spring reassembly; hover solos a stem), but its copy is now
inventory, not cinema:

> **One master. Five stems. Every cutdown. Cleared worldwide. Named so your
> editor never asks.**
> `AURORA_Anthem_60_MASTER_v3_FINAL`

**COMMERCIAL NOTE.** This beat is the homepage's load-bearing wall and the
15-second realization: the visitor has now *handled* the deliverable. Camera
grammar unchanged from the script.

### BEAT 4 · "THE SYSTEM" — why it comes out like that every time
**STORY.** Rack focus from the reassembled master to the surface behind it:
the ChordOS panels, working unattended — intelligence filling in, clearance
flipping to CLEARED, readiness ticking 8/9 → 9/9. Copy:

> **It ships like that because software runs it.**
> **Briefs become specs. Rights get cleared and certified. Versions name
> themselves. Nothing lives in someone's inbox.**

**MOTION.** The dolly-past-three-panels shot from the script, tightened to one
pass. Real product surfaces in the film grade — the months of ChordOS, on
camera, doing their job for the client's benefit (never as self-portrait).

### BEAT 5 · "YOUR WEEK, HANDLED" — confidence
**STORY.** The Beat-1 fragments return and are answered one-for-one — kept from
the script but compressed to a single scrolled pass, copy as flat statements:

> *"license fell through"* → **Original music. Nothing to re-license, ever.**
> *"v9_FINAL_final2"* → **v1 · v2 · v3 FINAL. A system names the files.**
> *"stems, today?"* → **Stems ship with the master. Always.**
> *"legal flagged it"* → **Sample-free, certified, on file.**
> *"by Friday"* → **Readiness is a checklist, not a feeling: 9/9.**

Off-grid chaos dissolving into on-beat answers — the tension mechanic survives,
at half the runtime.

### BEAT 6 · "THE INVITATION"
**STORY.** Still frame, record light breathing, nothing needing anything.

> **Send us the campaign.**
> *Deadline included. We reply with an approach and a price range.*

One magnetic button: **Send the campaign →**. Footer: one program line. End.

## Commercial pace laws (new, binding)
- Reassurance by **0:06**. Evidence in hand by the **first scroll**. Product
  named inside the hero.
- Every scene must answer an EP question (*what do I get / how / why safe /
  what now*) — a scene that only displays craft is cut, however beautiful.
- The studio never appears without the client's problem in frame.
- Total intended runtime to CTA: **≤ 90 seconds** of engaged scrolling.
