## Rulings applied since the roadmap was drafted

The roadmap was written before the CEO Integration Review (`docs/ceo-integration-review.md`)
and the Brand Foundation (`docs/brand/BRAND_FOUNDATION.md`) landed. Four of their rulings
change it, and the brief records them as canon:

1. **Chordential is a music house.** ChordOS is not sold, pitched or put on a client surface
   until ten engagements and $250,000. The roadmap's "we market the process" stands, but
   the process is *how the studio works*, shown through its artefacts — never named as
   software.
2. **"Procurement-grade" is internal-only.** The roadmap led with it client-facing. Struck.
   The category is *music house*; the position inside it is *the one that finishes*.
3. **"Finished" leads; the certificate is enumerated inside it.** Clearance is not the
   headline. The SEO content still answers clearance queries, because that is what a
   producer types; the brand does not lead with them.
4. **The demand engine is stood down for sixty days in favour of thirty named people.**
   Every channel below runs at founder-only capacity and in service of those thirty
   conversations. Nothing here is a substitute for making them.

## 00 · The one decision
We market the process, because the process is what a buyer can verify before hiring us:
a signed Discovery Summary, a certificate bound to its document, one room, one round per
version, a manifest. A studio with no case studies still has a process; ours is legible.
Every recommendation passes one test: does it let a producer who has never met us verify
something? If it only asks them to believe us, it is cut.

## 01 · Positioning
See `00-brief.md` §1. Category: music house. Position: the one that finishes. Buyer: the
agency producer / head of production answering the creative director, business affairs
and finance at once.

## 02 · Messaging
Three pillars — Original · Cleared · Delivered — each with a proof surface, an objection
it answers, and a vocabulary (see the brief). The elevator, the objection table and the
never-say list are in the brief.

## 03 · The 90-day cue sheet
| Cue | Weeks | Job | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| m01 Foundation | 1–2 | One line everywhere; front door rewritten; **front-door honesty fixes** (placeholder-audio disclosure, demo data labelled); analytics stood up; LinkedIn profile built | a stranger can repeat what we do and for whom |
| m02 Proof surfaces | 3–4 | Certificate walkthrough (AURORA), the room in 90s, the manifest opened on camera, the licence-terms glossary | three linkable artefacts |
| m03 LinkedIn live | 5–8 | Three posts/week, daily comments, 15 connections/week from the named list | 12 posts up; 3 replies from target roles |
| m04 **The thirty** | 1–10 | The thirty named people, Southeast first; ten conversations a week; the five-touch sequence; the Clearance Read as the offer | 30 sequences started, 5 Reads delivered, 3 discovery calls, **one founding engagement** |
| m05 First breakdown | 9–12 | A real job end to end with permission, or a self-commissioned invented-brand film stated as such | published; sent as touch four |
| m06 Measure, cut, re-cue | 12–13 | Stop anything with no leading signal in 8 weeks; write the next cue sheet | the next sheet has fewer cues |

m04 is not a cue among six; it is the sixty-day ruling, and the others exist to make its
conversations land.

## 04 · Content — six series, three posts a week
How Agencies Actually Buy Music (weekly) · What Actually Happens After Approval? (weekly)
· Producer's Notebook (weekly, founder) · Music Myths (fortnightly) · Why This Commercial
Works (fortnightly, Q2) · Behind the Brief / Campaign Breakdown (monthly, from the first
delivered job). Quarterly themes: Q1 clearance answered (not led with) · Q2 the process ·
Q3 the business of music for advertising · Q4 our own numbers, published.

## 05–06 · LinkedIn and the weekly session log
Headline, About, Featured, cadence, connection and comment doctrine: brief §5. Mon buy /
Wed after-approval / Fri notebook; Tue and Thu comments only; monthly long-form.

## 07–12 · Community, free opportunities, partners, speaking, podcasts, relationships
Be the person in the room who knows how clearance works. Ten contributions before any
link. Two communities, twice a week. Partners are whoever hands music to a producer:
production companies, editorial, mix studios, freelance supervisors, localisation. One
talk, given until it is good. Relationships are built from public credits, never from
memory — six archetypes, sixty names by the end of m03.

## 13 · Outreach — five touches, six weeks
Day 0 connect (their thing, not ours) · day 7 value, no ask · day 17 the Clearance Read ·
day 30 the breakdown · day 42 the small ask, then stop; a no is a no for six months. Ten
new sequences a week, Tue–Thu mornings.

## 14 · SEO — own the questions a producer types
Six pillars in order: licence terms explained · what business affairs checks · original
vs library vs machine-made · what a delivery contains · how to brief a composer · what
original music costs and why. Four ungated downloads. No invented search volumes.

## 15 · KPIs — measure trust
Lagging: discovery calls (with source), signed Discovery Summaries, unsolicited inbound,
referrals. Leading: **Clearance Reads requested**, **replies from target roles**, saves and
shares on the artefacts, organic search to the pillars, connection acceptance rate. No
followers on the dashboard. Weekly sheet, Friday, fifteen minutes.

## What we will not do
No engagement bait. No fabricated proof. No machine-made music, including as a demo. No
paid growth in year one. No fourth post a week. No pitching in a community before ten
useful contributions. No naming the software to a client.
