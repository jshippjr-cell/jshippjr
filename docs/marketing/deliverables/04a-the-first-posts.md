# The first posts, and the profile

*Written 2026-09-10 from the founder's own account of why Chordential exists, given in
interview. Reads under `00-brief.md`; the LinkedIn seat's doctrine is in
`04-linkedin-growth.md`, and this file supersedes its section 6 as the copy of record.
Every line here is what the founder actually posts, so it is the highest-stakes copy in
the marketing folder: it is the first thing a producer sees after a connection request.*

**The founder's own facts, in his words (2026-09-10).** He is not a working composer. He
has ideas daily, used to compose, stopped, says he lacks the discipline to finish them,
and has a folder full of unfinished things. His motive is to own the place where the
people he admires get their work out — he says he could have been good at composing
and lacked the discipline, and what he values in them is the tenacity and focus he
didn't have. Not their sacrifice: their staying power. He chose advertising because that is
where the budget to pay them still is. He tried to buy a recording studio in 2025, asked
for financials, was refused, offered to operate it for a year at rent so both sides could
see real numbers, was refused again, and walked. His long aim is a 2,500 to 3,000 square
foot room built for chamber orchestra where a composer dials in and conducts remotely.
The digital business exists to earn that room.

### Wednesday 16 Sep — one thing the room does

> **If you've ever had a music note travel through an email thread, you know the shape of it.**
>
> "Can we try the thing at 0:42 again?"
>
> Except it was 0:44. And it was on v2, and we're on v3 now. And the composer read it as the whole passage, not the hit.
>
> In our room you click the bar at 0:42 and the music stops. You type what you heard. The note is pinned to that second, on that version, and it stays there. Drag it if you meant 0:44.
>
> Nobody has to remember what anyone meant.
>
> That's from a demonstration on an invented brand. There's no client work to show yet.

## The rule these were rewritten under

Three drafts failed before this one, and each failure has a rule now.

1. **Never define a word to the reader.** The About once read *"Composed means written for
   this campaign…"*. A producer knows what composed means; being told puts the writer above
   the reader and reads as marketing. Say what we do for them instead: *we write it for your
   campaign*. Active, ours, theirs — never a definition.
2. **Never write from a chair the founder does not sit in.** Four early posts gave vendor
   advice about delivering music, which he has not yet done commercially. He is a buyer of
   production building the house he wanted. Everything comes from that seat.
3. **Never about money** (brief §7).

The test for any new post: would a producer nod, and does it sound like him rather than
like a company?
