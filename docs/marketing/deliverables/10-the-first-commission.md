## 1. The brief

> **DEMONSTRATION. HALVARD is an invented brand, Pike & Rowan an invented agency, "Long Way Home"
> an invented campaign. No client commissioned this and none has paid for it. Chordential
> commissioned it and paid for it.**

The fiction is already in this repository and already labelled: `scripts/build_call_scripts.py`
builds a full discovery call for HALVARD, *"Long Way Home"*, Pike & Rowan. Reusing it gives intake
a script instead of an improvisation, re-scoped from that script's 2:00 master to the **:60
broadcast cut**: what one composer and one founder's own money can carry.

**Client** HALVARD — technical outdoor apparel, twenty years old, reads as cold. **Agency** Pike &
Rowan. **Producer** Marisa Del Rio. **Day-to-day approval** Aziz Barr, creative director. **Final
approval** Tom Vasquez, HALVARD's brand director — sees it once, at the end, not a music person.

**The film.** A woman walks home. The seasons change around her. No voiceover, almost no dialogue.
**The music is the narration.**

**Audience.** People who know the brand as competent and feel nothing about it. The job is to move
it from *reliable* to *meant something*. Marisa's measure: *"if the comments are about the film and
not about the price."*

**Tone.** Almost nothing to begin — one instrument, bare, cold, space between phrases. It warms; by
the last third it is full and lands somewhere hopeful. **Not triumphant.** The brand is careful
about anything reading as luxury; it is a working brand.

**References, in words.** Chase: chamber restraint — few real instruments, air around each, a
writer confident enough to leave four bars alone; low strings carrying weight without volume; a
melody hummable after one pass. Avoid: weightless wash with no bottom and no pulse; glossy
aspirational-corporate; a swell that arrives because the picture asked rather than because the
music earned it.

**What the music does, by the second (:60 hero, 24 fps, start TC 01:00:00:00).**

| From | To | What it must do |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 0:08 | One instrument, alone. No pulse. Cold. Nothing announces itself. |
| 0:08 | 0:22 | A second voice underneath. First hint of a pulse — felt, not heard. |
| 0:22 | 0:36 | The melody states itself plainly, once. Still small. |
| 0:36 | 0:38 | **The turn.** She reaches the ridge. Opens by one layer, not three. |
| 0:38 | 0:52 | Full, warm, moving. The section the :30 is cut from. |
| 0:52 | 0:58 | Resolve down, not up. Hopeful, not triumphant. |
| 0:58 | 1:00 | One held note under the end card. No button, no stinger. |

**Deliverables.** :60 hero · :30, :15 cutdowns · :06 bumper · four 9:16 verticals · stems by group
(not instrument) on the master only · tempo and key noted. Formats per clause 2A: **48 kHz /
24-bit WAV**, stereo master, stems from the same session, time-aligned. Loudness per
`service_agreement.LOUDNESS_TARGETS`: **US broadcast −24 LKFS ±2 (ATSC A/85), true peak −2 dBTP;
streaming −14 LUFS, true peak −1 dBTP**.

**Rounds.** Two. One round buys one version. A conform — the picture moved — is free.

**Licence terms it is written under.** Broadcast and organic social; no cinema, no out-of-home.
North America. Twelve months from first air. Category-exclusive in outdoor apparel for the term.
Publishing per the standing agreement — the composition assigned to Chordential Music, **50% of the
publisher's share belongs to the writers**, the writer keeps their writer's share of performance
income. **No figure appears**: §13.10 is open, so until the founder rules a band is not public and
the script's fictional budget stays in the script.

**Picture.** Jon cuts the :60 from licensed stock — landscape, a figure walking, seasons — and
delivers it with its frame rate and start timecode written down, the one thing his own outbound
checklist asks of every vendor. No real trademark on screen; the end card is the invented wordmark
set in type.

## 2. Who writes it

**Profile one from `09-composer-track.md` §2 — the spot composer, to picture, discipline
`MusicDiscipline.COMPOSITION`.** `agreements.kind_for` reads that discipline and returns the
**Composer Agreement**, the document this demonstration is about; a mixer signs the Service
Agreement — a fee, no publishing — and demonstrates none of it. The producer's question is about
music against picture, and only this profile answers it. And 09 names it as Jon's own gap: *"none
in the style I'd hire."*

**One person, not two.** A separate mix engineer doubles the paper and the money and halves the
clarity of what is proven. Ask for a mixed master.

**What their work must show.** A reel of music **against picture**, not a page of tracks. Two
released spots or brand films where **they wrote the cue** — not arranged, not mixed. One idea
existing at more than one length: a hook that survives a :06 is a different skill from one that
works at :60, and it is what gets music killed in the edit. Restraint. And the loudness numbers in
conversation, not looked up — they bounce to a broadcast target themselves.

`talent.matchable` needs an **approved reel and a recorded discipline**, so a name is not a roster
row until Jon has listened and pressed `/talent/{id}/review`.

**Where he finds them**, from 09 §3 and §6, in order: **trade credit boxes on Southeast work** —
SHOOT, Little Black Book, Muse by Clio *(verify each publishes composer credits)*: the **music**
credit on spots of the last 24 months, and the house named there lists its writers. **VI-Control**
work threads filtered to `commercial`, `advertising`, `:30 spot`. **Frost Media Scoring &
Production**, Miami — email the department: *which alumni of the last ten years work in advertising
music?* Local, so able to come to a session later; alumni, not students. **Recording Academy
Florida Chapter**, in person. Ten rows, each with a public source URL; then three messages a day.

## 3. The approach

Different from 09's message one in one way: **there is a brief and there is money.** The composer
reviewer wrote the shape himself (§4): *"four sentences: what the brief is, what the fee is and
what it is a percentage of, that no client has paid yet, and where the terms are."*

**Subject:** A paid commission — :60, no client, my own money

> {first},
>
> I found {piece} through {where}. {One specific thing actually heard — the arrangement choice, the
> place they left space, the instrument that carries it.}
>
> I'm commissioning one piece and I'd like it to be yours. A :60 brand film for an invented outdoor
> brand — no voiceover, the music is the narration, a turn at 0:38, plus :30, :15, :06 and four
> verticals. The brief is attached in full.
>
> **There is no client. There has never been one.** I run a small music house in Miami and I'm
> paying for this myself so there is one real piece of work on the site instead of a description of
> one. That is the entire reason it exists, and you should price my seriousness accordingly.
>
> **The fee is ${fee}, flat, in writing before you accept, paid inside 30 days of delivery** — not
> 120, because there is no client to wait on. My standing agreement pays a writer 30% of net
> creative revenue, 40% with the session; there is no client revenue here, so a share of it would
> be a share of nothing, and a flat fee stated up front is the honest form. If the piece is ever
> licensed to a real client, the same 30% of the net of that payment reaches you for as long as it
> earns — that clause is in the agreement and I'll write the variation that applies it here.
>
> **What you keep either way:** your writer's share of performance income, paid direct by the PRO.
> Half the publisher's share. Your name on the cue sheet I file. Your reel rights the day I publish
> it.
>
> **What I'm not offering:** a job after this one, a client, volume, or exclusivity over your time
> — ever. This is one piece.
>
> I'll send you the whole agreement to read, unsigned, before I ask you for anything, and I'll walk
> you through what a note looks like when it reaches you here. Twenty minutes.
>
> Jon Shipp · Chordential · Miami

**On the fee**, §7 lifts the money rule for a creator: terms **as terms, never as generosity** — a
number and a clause, no adjectives, never comparative. **It must not promise** work after this, a
client, volume, first-look, a rate card, an air date or a case study; must not describe the
certificate at all, because counsel has not reviewed the indemnity language (Ruling 4); and must
not mention the chamber-orchestra room, which the composer reviewer says inverts the order — he
reads the room, then reads the terms through it, and discounts both. Two shipped lines must stop
being false first (09's outstanding correction): `recruiting.py`'s *"real, paid briefs (never
spec)"* and `for_artists.html`'s *"We bring you scoped, paid work"*.

## 4. The demonstration, staged

**Order matters more than content**: brief and fee first, in writing; then the room. A room shown
before a fee reads as software being sold to someone who wants to write.

**Moment 1 — in the email, no screens.** The agreement, whole and unsigned. Jon creates the talent
row, records `COMPOSITION`, mints the portal token (`/talent/{id}/portal`) and sends the link to
**`/creator/{token}/agreement`**, which renders `composer_agreement.signable_text()` in full — the
text a signature binds to — with the limit underneath: *"This is a standing agreement, not a
booking. It commits you to no work and guarantees you none."* **Gap:** no public URL shows this
text before someone is a talent row, so the composer reviewer's number-one request is a route that
does not exist.

**Moment 2 — on the call, twelve minutes, screen shared.** Four screens, this order.

1. **The brief in the room** — `/room/{project_id}?t={token}`, layer **`B`**: objective,
   references, tone, deliverables, deadline (`delivery.seed_brief`), and the cue list with its hits
   — what the music does at which second, in the product, not in a PDF. *Answers: will the target
   move under me.*
2. **The Notes layer, `N`** — a note pinned at a timecode, the round chip (`round_phrase`), and the
   client's two buttons beside each other: **✓ Approve** and **↺ Ask for a new version**. The
   room's own copy, verbatim: *"Notes are free, and they are the conversation"*; *"Approving does
   not spend a round… Ask for a new version spends one — leaving notes never does."* Plus the rule
   the copy does not say: a second change request while the studio still owes a version is
   **refused server-side**.
3. **The same room from the client's side.** A note the composer wrote is signed **"Chordential"** —
   `room.CAPS[CLIENT]` has no `see_who`, so `room_view` rewrites the author and blanks
   `from_creator` on every row of the version ladder. The client's checklist has the roster names
   taken out and the budget removed (`readiness_view` strips rows tagged `lens == "commercial"`).
   *Answers: does the client learn who I am, or what I cost. No, and no.*
4. **The disposition queue** — `console_routes.disposition_queue`. Three undispositioned notes. Jon
   calls one a **conform** (free), one a **revision** (spends a round), one **out of scope**
   (quoted, never actioned for nothing). Reload the composer's token view and the out-of-scope note
   **is not there** — `room.priced_notes_only` keeps only `conform` and `revision` — while the room
   still says one note is with the studio (`unpriced_n`), because *"they said nothing"* and *"they
   said something and the studio has it"* are different sentences.

**That last one is the moment.** Watching a piece of unpaid work get stopped before it reaches you
is what a burned composer has never seen anybody do, and it takes forty seconds to show.

**Noise to a creator, and it stays closed:** the buyer graph, qualification and scoring, the
estimator's margin lines, `/pricing`, the package's ZIP layout, the manifest, the front door's
scroll world, anything with *intelligence* on it.

**Second-hardest:** the 120-day backstop paid whether or not the client paid, as a real payout row;
3C's continuing 30% on every later payment to widen the licence; 3B's closed deduction list.

| 15 | Payment | Pays the fee | The payout row |

**The gaps.**

1. **No client signature, deliberately.** ADR-0067's road to a project runs through a countersigned
   proposal. There is no client, and manufacturing one would forge a record under the module whose
   whole purpose is that records are real — `landing.py` refuses exactly this on the sample page.
   **The signature path is the one part of the product this run does not exercise.**
2. **Kickoff and the deposit have no payer.** The invoice stays outstanding and the download stays
   locked. **Never** use `/pay/return` — an unauthenticated GET that marks an invoice Paid with no
   verification against Stripe, known and live. The honest unlock is `/delivery/unlock`.
3. **Two pay models, and the one the composer signed loses.** `db.ensure_project_payouts` pays the
   flat `talent.rate` when one is on file, falling back to the 30% policy only when it is not; and
   `db._writer_fee_per_head` computes the share from `est.suggested_price`, the figure
   `pricing.build_quote` deliberately does not use. Here the flat fee **is** the agreed fee, so the
   ledger happens to be right. It will not be on the second job.
4. **No "accept this engagement" action.** Clause 1 says an engagement is accepted *"when the
   writer accepts it in the studio's system"*; the only record is the assignment row, and
   `recruiting.compose_project_assignment` mails a **rate**, not the fee and its basis. Send scope
   and fee by hand before assigning.
5. **Agreed variations cannot be signed through the product.** `agreements.build_for` calls
   `build_agreement(talent_row)` with no `notes`, so the AGREED VARIATIONS block is unreachable
   from the signing route. The two this job needs — 4B's reel rights vesting on **Chordential's
   publication** rather than a client's release, and 6C's competing-brand restraint disapplied
   because there is no brand to protect — go in the written offer.
6. **No cue sheet to file.** Clause 7 promises filing within 30 days of first broadcast; there is
   none. It is built and published as an artefact, nothing is filed, and the page says so.

## 7. Cost, time, order — and the risks

**Money. All (est.), reasoned.**

| Line | (est.) | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Composer fee, flat | **$3,000** | `compensation.py` calibrates the whole share policy against it: *"30% of net on a :30 national spot is $2,980 against the $3,000 the estimator already modelled."* The one number Jon can defend as not invented |
| Mixed master | **$0** | Asked of the same composer, inside the fee. A separate engineer adds ~$700 (`ROLE_RATES["Mix Engineer"]` $125/h × ~6h) and a second agreement |
| One live player, remote | **$400**, optional | Two hours; most improves the piece and most proves *made by people*. Cut first if money is tight |
| Stock footage | **$300** | 8–12 clips, standard licence, no trademarks on screen |
| Counsel on the certificate | **$700–1,500**, separate | §13.2 calls it a budget item before composer number one. Needed before the certificate is described in public as anything |
| **Total, lean / as planned** | **$3,300 / $3,700** | ± the player |

**Hours (est.), against his ~20 a week.** Brief and picture 12h · finding and messaging composers
6h (shares 09's four supply hours) · calls and the offer 3h · running the job 10h · publishing and
the walkthrough recording 8h. **≈39 hours over six weeks, ≈6–7 a week** — inside the twenty,
displacing build hours, never the selling hours Ruling 5 requires to exceed them.

**Week 0** fix the two false lines, write the brief, and rehearse the four screens on seeded demo
data (`CHORDENTIAL_SEED_DEMO=1` carries a mid-revision job with notes) so nothing is discovered
live. **Week 1** cut the picture, build the ten composer rows, decide the fee and write the
engagement offer with its variations. **Week 2** message one, three a day; two calls, the
demonstration on both. **Week 3** offer accepted, row approved, agreement signed and countersigned,
assigned; brief, picture and cues into the room. **Week 4** v1 lands — notes, dispositions, one
round. **Week 5** v2, approval, creative lock, cutdowns and verticals. **Week 6** certificate, cue
sheet, package; publish; pay.

**Before the first message:** the brief is written and the picture is cut — you cannot offer a
commission with nothing attached; the fee is decided and in writing; the four screens have been
walked once on demo data; the two false shipped lines are fixed; and Jon has read the agreement end
to end as a composer would, writing down every question he cannot answer.

**Risks.**

- **The first composer says no.** Likely, and not a signal. Go three deep. If all three decline the
  fee or the brief is wrong — **raise the fee before weakening the brief**, because a weak brief
  produces a weak piece and a weak piece is worse than none.
- **The piece comes back weak.** The real risk, because it answers the producer's only question in
  the wrong direction. Clause 2A gives five working days to accept or say in writing what is wrong,
  revisions inside the stated rounds. If it is still not good enough after them, 2B applies — **the
  writer is paid in full and the piece is not published.** The money is spent either way; the
  recovery is a second commission, not a re-cut.
- **The brief is what is wrong.** Have someone who commissions music read it first — a peer or a
  referral, never one of the thirty; a target asked for a favour is spent.
- **The demonstration lands as software.** Mitigated by order, and by twelve minutes.
- **Scope creep into a build.** Six gaps are named above and none is fixed. The freeze holds.
- **Publishing overreach.** No claim the certificate is guaranteed or indemnified until counsel has
  read it. No "case study". No implication a client exists.
