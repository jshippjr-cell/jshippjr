# Social Media Manager — deliverable

*Wave 3, 2026-09-09. Reads under `docs/marketing/00-brief.md`, then 01, 02, 05 and 07;
the brief wins any difference. LinkedIn has its own seat (04); this file owns YouTube,
Instagram, X and TikTok, and the cross-platform calendar. Revenue is zero: no client, case
study or testimonial. Every demonstration is AURORA, Vance Athletic or HALVARD, labelled.
No machine-made music is heard anywhere, including the current placeholders (decision a);
the customer promise is never a headline (decision b).*

**Two readings this file rests on.** (1) "Three posts a week" counts things *written*. A
cut of one of them to an archive or a grid is the same post again, per the Content
Strategist's cascade. If the CEO reads it otherwise, every Instagram still moves onto the
day of its parent post and the cascade's lag is lost — nothing else changes. (2) A room
recording is muted, with a caption saying so, until (a) resolves. On YouTube that is an
honest archive. On Instagram, whose only job is a legitimacy check, a silent Reel from a
music house fails the check, so the Reel is **held, not cut**, until human-made
demonstration music exists.

## 1. Per platform

**LinkedIn — coordination only.** The founder's profile is the canonical surface for
all three weekly posts; 04 writes them. What moves off it: the Wednesday recording file,
unchanged, to YouTube the same day; the Monday Myth image and the Behind the Brief
stated/assumed table to Instagram as stills, one to four weeks later; nothing from the
Notebook, ever. Nothing moves *onto* LinkedIn from another platform, and YouTube and
Instagram carry no post LinkedIn did not.

**YouTube — archive only.** *Role:* the Wednesday recording sits where search finds it
in a year. No audience is sought; subscribers are not counted. *Tone:* the title is the
LinkedIn post's claim; the description is factual and short. *Cadence:* one upload a
Wednesday, from week 3, ten minutes of founder time. *Formats:* the same ≤90-second file,
no separate production, no thumbnail beyond a typographic card from the Myth template,
one playlist named for the series, chapters filled at upload. *Public or unlisted:*
public — an archive nobody can find is not one. A recording is **unlisted** only while it
shows a surface where a demo record is still unlabelled (brief §8.2) or where placeholder
audio could be heard; the URL never changes, so flipping it public later loses nothing.
The two *written* Wednesdays (weeks 1–2) have no file and go nowhere. *Never there:*
Shorts, a channel trailer, end-screen asks, a "subscribe" line, anything with sound or a
narrator until (a) resolves, any roster name.

**Instagram — minimal.** *Role:* a legitimacy check — a producer who looks us up finds
a grid of artefacts, not a void. *Tone:* the image carries the sentence; the caption
adds the source and the label. *Cadence:* **at most one post a week and four a month**,
every one a cut of something already on LinkedIn, scheduled in one thirty-minute
session a month. *Formats:* a 1080×1350 typographic still (the Myth image; the
stated/assumed table), alt text written; the Wednesday cut as a Reel once (a) resolves;
the wordmark as profile image, the founder named in the bio. *Never there:* Stories,
carousels, a photo of a session that never happened, hashtags (reach is not a metric),
library music under a still, a "link in bio" line, a post that exists only here.

**X and TikTok — closed, year one.** Closed means: the handle is claimed
(**@chordential**, verify availability on each), the profile carries the wordmark, a bio
and — from week 3, when the front door is honest — the site link; **no posting, no
following, no replies, no reposts**, notifications off, a monthly check for
impersonation. A mention from a tier 1–3 person is answered by email. The same holds
for Threads and Facebook pages.

*X bio (≤160):* "A music house. Original music for campaigns — composed, cleared,
delivered. Not active here; Jon Shipp reads every brief at chordential.com."
*TikTok bio (≤80):* "A music house. Original music for campaigns. Not active here."
*Instagram bio (≤150):* "A music house. Original music for campaigns — composed, cleared,
delivered. Founder: Jon Shipp. Every brand shown here is invented."

## 3. The first ten posts

### YouTube — archive copies of Recordings 1–10 (series: *What Actually Happens After Approval?*)

Every description ends with the same three lines, not repeated below: *"AURORA is an
invented brand; this is a demonstration, not client work. The recording is muted: the
demonstration music is not yet recorded by a person, and we do not play what we did not
make. Recorded from the room by Jon Shipp, Chordential — a music house."* One link per
description, to a pillar page that exists by then (SEO §2).

1. **You pressed Approve. Here is what it locked · After Approval 1.** The list beside
   the button, read aloud by the cursor: approving locks the master, spends no round,
   releases no payment, and can be undone. Chapters: the take · the list · Approve · undo.
   Link: `/learn/what-a-delivery-contains`.
2. **The room in 90 seconds · After Approval 2.** One link, one campaign: the doorline,
   the take, a note pinned to a second, the two buttons, the checklist. Chapters by
   layer: B · N · V · C. Link: `/learn/what-a-delivery-contains`.
3. **A note is not a round · After Approval 3.** Click into the bar; the music pauses;
   *Note left at 0:42 on v2*. The pin drags; Range marks a passage. Notes cost nothing
   and stay with that version. Chapters: pin · drag · range · reply. Link:
   `/learn/how-to-brief-a-composer`.
4. **Ask for a new version — what a round buys · After Approval 4.** The second button
   opens its box only when chosen and says it spends a round. While one is owed the room
   says *We're already writing your next version*. Chapters: the button · the box · the
   gate. Link: `/learn/what-original-music-costs`.
5. **The note is priced before anyone writes · After Approval 5.** Conform, revision or
   out of scope, decided by the studio in the room; the composer sees *3 client notes
   with the studio* — a count, never the words — until then. Chapters: the note · the
   studio's call · the composer's view. Link: `/learn/what-original-music-costs`.
6. **V — every take, numbered, nothing pending · After Approval 6.** Published versions
   only; a take the studio has not heard is never in the client's list. Chapters: v1 ·
   v2 · what is not there. Link: `/learn/what-a-delivery-contains`.
7. **B — the brief the composer reads · After Approval 7.** Objective, tone, references
   as a direction, agreed directions, *What you're buying*. Anything the brief did not
   state is marked assumed. Chapters: objective · references · directions · assumed.
   Link: `/learn/how-to-brief-a-composer`.
8. **C — roles, not names · After Approval 8.** Where this stands: the checklist, and the
   team as roles — *composer: assigned*. The client sees the state; the studio sees the
   people. Chapters: the checklist · roles · why not names. Link:
   `/learn/what-business-affairs-checks`.
9. **Sign off each deliverable · After Approval 9.** After approval every file is signed
   off one at a time — master, cutdowns, stems, cue sheet — and the package holds only
   what was. Chapters: the list · one sign-off · the package fills. Link:
   `/learn/what-a-delivery-contains`.
10. **Cleared & original — the certificate · After Approval 10.** *(Only once the
    clearance line is computed from the releases on file, brief §8.3; else Rec 11, the
    package and the manifest, takes this slot.)* Media, territory, term, exclusivity,
    publishing, stated; a signature bound to the text that reports *no longer matches* if
    a term changes. Chapters: the five lines · the releases · the signature. Link:
    `/learn/what-business-affairs-checks`. Never *guaranteed*, never *indemnified*.

## 4. Comment and reply doctrine

**YouTube and Instagram** (open platforms; comments on). Jon replies himself, in one
sitting on Friday, within the week; a question from a tier 1–3 role is answered within a
working day. Order: a target role asking a question; anyone asking a licence, delivery
or cue-sheet question; a composer asking about the roster (one sentence pointing to
the composers' page); everyone else, thanks or nothing. A reply never: sells, names the
Clearance Read or a call unless asked directly, names a competitor, a library or a
machine-music company, gives legal advice ("ask your counsel" is the sentence), claims or
implies a client, argues past one exchange, or answers *"is this AI?"* with more than
the one sentence. **Thanks, not a pitch:** praise gets thanks and, at most, an answer to
a question the commenter actually asked. *Who is the client* gets the truth: an invented
brand, a demonstration, no client yet. Spam and vendor pitches are deleted without
reply. A target-role question that deserves more than a comment moves to email and is
logged for the outbound seat — never to a DM thread. No link in a reply; the description
carries the one link.

**X and TikTok** — closed; no comments to answer. A mention or DM from a target role is
answered by email once, the same week; the platform is not opened to do it.

**LinkedIn** — 04's doctrine governs; a comment there asking for a recording is
answered with the YouTube archive title, not a link.

## 5. The TikTok decision

**No, year one — defended.** The buyer is an agency producer, a head of production or
business affairs; none of them buys music, or is asked *who do you use*, on TikTok. A
music house cannot post there without sound, and no human-made demonstration exists yet.
Every founder hour there is an hour not on the thirty (Ruling 5), and a delivery week
would kill the cadence anyway. What would reopen it, all four together: (1) a named
tier 1–3 person on the list is found active there *in their professional role*, by
public evidence; (2) human-made demonstration music exists; (3) a founding engagement
is signed and founder marketing hours have stayed under eight for eight consecutive
weeks; (4) the m06 review re-cues the demand engine. If reopened: a twelve-week test of
Wednesday cuts only, nothing made for it, under the brief's stop rule — no reply or Read
request from a target role in eight weeks → closed again, the handle kept.

## 6. What I cut, and why

- **X twice a week; the TikTok Q3 test** — withdrawn by brief §10; the handles are held.
- **The Instagram Reel until (a) resolves** — a silent music-house Reel fails the one job
  the account has. YouTube keeps the muted copy because an archive can be silent honestly.
- **Hashtags** — reach devices; reach is not a metric, and a tag is the easiest thing to
  name without confirming.
- **Stories, carousels, Shorts, a channel trailer, custom thumbnails, posting-time
  optimisation, follower targets, paid boost, a link-in-bio service** — production or
  growth that costs founder hours and produces no qualified conversation.
- **Instagram-only or YouTube-only content** — the cascade is downward only; a platform
  that originates something is a fourth post by another route.
- **Any post that references, embeds or links the placeholder recordings** — decision (a).
- **The customer promise as an image** — decision (b); never a headline, so never a still.

### Objection to the brief

None. Two readings, stated in the preamble: a cut is not a fourth post, and the Instagram
Reel waits for human-made music. If the CEO rules either the other way, the preamble
carries the fallback and nothing else in this file moves.
