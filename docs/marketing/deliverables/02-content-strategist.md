*Wave 2, 2026-09-09. Reads under `docs/marketing/00-brief.md` and
`deliverables/01-brand-strategist.md`; they win where this differs. Revenue is zero: no
client, no case study, no testimonial. Every episode below is writable today from the
room, the certificate, the package, the pricing model and Jon Shipp's calls, on invented

Two open decisions constrain this; nothing depends on either: (a) the placeholder
recordings are machine-made, so nothing references, embeds or links them, and every room

## 1. The six series

**Displacement rule.** The fortnightly and monthly series displace a slot of their own
day; nothing is added. Music Myths alternates Mondays with
How Agencies Actually Buy Music; Behind the Brief takes one Friday a month from the
Notebook; Why This Commercial Works alternates Wednesdays from Q2. A displaced episode is
not made up.

### 1.1 How Agencies Actually Buy Music — Monday, alternate weeks
LinkedIn post ≤220 words, first line the claim. Pillar: Original. **Saved for:** the
question a producer asks their next music house.

1. **The music number is not the campaign number.** Why we ask for the *music* number by
   name, and whether it includes the licence.
2. **Does the term run from delivery or from first air?** Why it moves the fee.
3. **Exclusivity is the term everyone assumes is free.** Category versus full.
4. **Who isn't on the call but can send it back?** The absent approver, and the checkpoint
   that prevents a late direction change.

### 1.2 What Actually Happens After Approval? — Wednesday, weekly
Screen recording from the room, ≤90 seconds, AURORA marked *demonstration*, muted until
(a) resolves; archived to YouTube, a Reel on Instagram. Pillar: Delivered. **Saved for:**
the second approver who never sat on a call.

1. **You pressed Approve. Here is what it locked.** The "What approving commits" list:
   locks the master; spends no round; releases no payment; can be undone.
2. **A note is not a round.** The music pauses, the note is pinned to that second, the pin
   drags, a range marks a passage. Costs nothing.
3. **Ask for a new version — what a round buys.** One round buys one version, and the gate
   while one is owed: *"We're already writing your next version."*
4. **The note is priced before anyone writes.** Conform, revision or out of scope; the
   composer sees a count, never the words, until then.

### 1.3 Producer's Notebook — Friday, weekly
Jon, first person, 300–500 words: what changed this week and why. **Saved for:** the
sentence a producer repeats to a creative director. The lesson must have happened, about
our process, never a client; copy-edited only.

1. **Zero.** What we have on the day we start selling, and what we do not.
2. **What I got wrong about pricing.** The quote used to echo the budget; now two fees and
   a verdict.
3. **Why the second button says "Ask for a new version".** A client read "request changes"
   as leaving a note; what the room now says beside the button.
4. **Why the client never hears a take we haven't.** The studio listens first.

### 1.4 Music Myths — Monday, alternate weeks
≤150 words plus one typographic image: the belief struck through, the correction, the
source. Pillar: Cleared. **Saved for:** the indexed list checked before signing. Legal
claims are reviewed before publication.

1. **"Royalty-free means free of copyright."** It waives royalties, not ownership; a claim
   can still follow the post **(verify platform terms)**.
2. **"The platform's music library covers our paid posts."** Native use only; paid spend
   and reposts fall outside it **(verify)**.
3. **"If a machine made it, anyone can use it."** Nothing to license, nothing to defend.
   US Copyright Office (Jan 2025), federal appeals court (Mar 2025) **(re-verify before use)**.
4. **"A quote should land on the budget."** A number equal to the budget is an echo.

### 1.5 Why This Commercial Works — Wednesday, alternate weeks from Q2
A public campaign by anyone, credited to its house, never implying involvement,
inferences labelled, never disparaging. **Saved for:** the decision map of a spot people
have seen. Spots are chosen the week before under the verify rule; the first four are
titled by mechanism.

1. **The hero element.** The idea that is the track, and how it survives the cutdowns.
2. **Where the music was told what to do.** Reading the sync points.
3. **One track, three lengths.** What a :30, :15 and :06 keep and lose.
4. **What the licence had to cover.** Where it ran implies media, territory, term —
   inference, never a figure.

### 1.6 Behind the Brief — one Friday a month
LinkedIn article 900–1,400 words; an invented brief, labelled in the first paragraph,
priced through the real quote builder and shown as a band with what was assumed beside it.
**Saved for:** the stated-versus-assumed table. **Becomes Campaign Breakdown** at the first
delivered job (m05), with permission or self-commissioned and stated as such.

1. **AURORA, a :60 brand film: what the brief said, what we had to assume.**
2. **HALVARD, a holiday spot: the reference they were trying to kill.** Direction agreed
   before composition.
3. **Vance Athletic, a social sprint: the brief that said "everywhere".** What one word
   does to media and territory.
4. **The brief with a budget and no music number.** The verdict; the reduced scope offered.

## 3. The three assets

### 3.1 The pricing explainer — *How the number is built*
Site page ≤500 words, linked from the Discovery Summary. States **no number**; the
proposal is the only place a client sees one.

1. **Two fees, and why.** A creative fee is what it costs to make — writer, players, room,
   mix — at the studio's margin. A licence fee is what the *use* is worth. Separate because
   a cue costs the same to write whether it runs on one channel for a season or nationally
   for three years.
2. **What moves the licence.** **Media** (where it runs), **territory** (where in the
   world), **term** (how long, from when), **exclusivity** (nobody in your category, or
   nobody at all) — the one most often assumed free, priced before promised.
3. **The ceiling.** Four factors multiplied compound faster than the market, so the model
   carries a documented cap; a deal at the cap is told so.
4. **The floor.** We never quote below what it costs to make the work well. Below it we
   offer the reduced scope that fits, or say *not this one*, in writing.
5. **What the budget does.** Nothing to the price. It returns a verdict — below the floor,
   in band, above — and we tell you which.
6. **What was assumed.** Any licence input the brief did not state is named as assumed
   beside the number.
7. **What this is not.** Not a rate card. The factors are priors ratified against published
   research, not yet calibrated on our own jobs; we say so.
8. **Honest close.** Send the brief; Jon reads it; the Discovery Summary carries both fees.

### 3.2 The objection FAQ — *Questions producers ask us*
Site page; answers ≤80 words, mechanics not adjectives, conceding what is true, traced to
the claims register. Never *guaranteed*, *indemnified*, *on time*, *partner*, a response
time, insurance or archiving.

| Question | The answer's paragraph |
|---|---|
| Why not a library, or a generator? | A library is a file whose licence paid spend and reposts break; machine output has no author to own it. This is written for this campaign by a named person, and you hold the paper. Sometimes a library track is right; we say when. |
| Can we defend this on paid spend, cross-channel? | The Clearance Certificate states media, territory, term, exclusivity and publishing; releases are on file for every named contributor; the signature is bound to the text and reports *no longer matches* if a term changes. |
| What actually happens after approval? | Approve locks the master, spends no round, releases no money. You sign off each deliverable; the package carries masters, cutdowns, stems, cue sheet, certificate and a manifest naming every file. |
| Too expensive. / The other house quotes one number. | The comparators price one person's writing time. The music can be bought cheaper; the finish cannot. Their one number hides a licence; ours shows it, so term, territory and exclusivity are yours to move. |
| Your no-AI line is unenforceable. | We do not generate audio; the agreement and every creator's standing agreement say so. Nobody can watch a composer's laptop; the difference is a studio built for the question. |
| Who owns it? Publishing? | Stated in the Discovery Summary you sign and repeated on the certificate: the recording, the grant of rights, and publishing, which stays with the studio and is what the cue sheet files. |
| Your downside is unbounded. / What's your Friday-night story? | Agreed: read the paper first — a demonstration certificate and cue sheet — and start small. We are small; Jon is the contact; every note, version and rights fact is in the room at one link. |

### 3.3 The room walkthrough — *One session in the room*
A written page now; a narrated recording under three minutes when (a) resolves. AURORA,
labelled; no roster names. Eight beats, each a control that exists:

1. **One link.** The doorline: wordmark, the badge *Client*, the campaign, the round
   phrase, the state. It never changes.
2. **Before we start.** Pay the deposit; send us your cut with its frame rate and start
   timecode. *Your cut is not waiting on the money.*
3. **Say it at the moment you hear it.** Click into the bar; the music pauses; the note is
   pinned to that second — *Note left at 0:42 on v2.* Drag the pin; press Range for a passage.
4. **N — the notes.** Every note against this version, the studio's replies signed
   *Chordential*. Notes are free and are the conversation.
5. **The note is priced.** Off camera, the studio calls it a conform (free), a revision (a
   round) or out of scope (quoted); the composer sees *3 client notes with the studio*
   until then.
6. **Two buttons decide the take.** The list above says what approving commits. **Approve**
   locks the master, spends no round, can be undone. **Ask for a new version** opens its box
   only when chosen, says it spends a round, and is not offered while we still owe one.
7. **V, B and C.** Every take, numbered, published only. The brief: objective, tone,
   references, directions, *What you're buying*. Where this stands: the checklist, and the
   team as roles — *composer: assigned* — never names.
8. **After approval.** Each deliverable signed off; *Cleared & original* — the certificate,
   its signature bound to the text; the package downloads when the final invoice clears.
   *Delivered. Everything you signed off is in your package.*

## 4. The editorial standard

A piece publishes when every line is true, answered in writing by the reviewer.

1. The one sentence — what a reader can now say — written before drafting, present in the piece.
2. Every claim has a dated primary source; legal claims a named reviewer; priced examples
   ran through the real quote builder and restate no number.
3. No client implied, by statement or omission; demonstration, composite and inference
   labelled where they occur; AURORA, Vance Athletic, HALVARD only.
4. No machine-made music heard, including the placeholders; any music heard is credited to
   its human composer.
5. Vocabulary checked by search against brief §6 and Brand Strategist §4; the certificate
   enumerated, never adjectivised; no named peer ranked; libraries sometimes right, said so.
6. Title is the claim; first line true alone; no hooks, bait, hype or countdown.
7. Honest close: one invitation, one link, or the Clearance Read named once a month. No
   pitch inside content.
8. Reads as Jon: present tense, past-participle promises; *not yet observed* is a sentence.
   Derivatives stand alone; others' work credited; anything unconfirmed **(verify)**.
9. Jon has read the final version, not a summary, and signed the ledger row.

A slot that cannot pass is skipped visibly and the reason logged.
