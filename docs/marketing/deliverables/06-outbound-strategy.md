## 2. The sequence: five touches, six weeks

Days count from touch one and land on the nearest **Tuesday–Thursday morning** on or
after the day. Every touch stands alone: no "following up", one page at most. Touches
two to five go by email where a work address is published, otherwise by message once the
connection is accepted; **no acceptance by day 7 and no address → the sequence pauses**
and restarts at day 0 if accepted later. Never two touches to one person in a week; never
tier C before their producer replies. The signature line is the Brand Strategist's for a
producer's inbox.

`{brand}` is the real brand on the public credit — their work, as published.

### The reply that ends the sequence

Any reply in their own words ends it — "thanks" counts, an auto-reply does not
(Analytics §1). From then on it is a conversation under the Relationship Strategy's
rules. Three replies
have their own rule: a **Read request** → §3; a **no, not now, we have people, stop** →
six months (§4); a **question about a job or a price** → the playbook's scoped call and
an `opportunities` row (§4). Silence through day 42 is not a no: the sequence closes at
day 49, the person goes Cold, and the only future touch is their event.

## 3. The Clearance Read, operationalised

**What it is.** One hour on a licence *they* hold, for music we did not make, returning
one page on what the paper covers and where it does not. Never a Read of our own
certificate, never a demo, never spec.

**How it is requested.** By reply — to touch three, in any conversation, on a call. No
form (the build is frozen, and a form is a funnel). The founder replies the same day with
the five questions and *"send the paper as a PDF or paste the text,"* and logs the
request that day (§4) — the leading metric; delivered Reads are a second column.

**Intake — five questions.** The Read compares paper against *use*, which the paper
rarely states:

1. Where is it running now, and where else might it — channels, cutdowns, in-store, the
   client's own accounts?
2. Which territories, and for how long — from delivery or from first air?
3. Is any of it paid — paid social, broadcast, pre-roll, out-of-home?
4. Who else may re-use it — the client's other agencies, a re-post, next year's refresh?
5. Has anything been flagged already — a claim, a question from the client's legal?

**The one page returned.** A fixed template, filled, never restructured:

1. **The paper.** One line: licensor, licence type (library / sync / custom), date, who
   signed. Nothing evaluated yet.
2. **What it grants.** Five lines — *media · territory · term · exclusivity · publishing*
   — each quoted from the paper where it speaks, or **not stated** where it is silent.
3. **Against your use.** The five answers, each marked **covered / not covered / not
   stated**, with the clause beside it.
4. **Where it doesn't reach.** The gaps in plain words: paid spend, cross-channel
   re-post, the term's end date, a territory not named, edits and cutdowns, client
   re-use. Only gaps the paper shows.
5. **Three questions to put to the licensor.** Verbatim, ready to forward.
6. **What I can't tell from this.** What needs counsel or the licensor. Last line, always:
   *This is a reading, not legal advice; confirm anything you act on with counsel.*

Signed with the founder's name and the date. **No spine, no signature line, no mention of
Chordential, the room, the certificate or what we do** — forwardable to their business
affairs with nothing on it that reads as a pitch.

**The hour.** Ten minutes on the intake, thirty on the paper, fifteen on the template,
five to send. It stays an hour because the template is
fixed; one licence per Read; an MSA or anything over ten pages gets its music schedule
read and the rest named under heading 6; the licensor is never called, the licence never
redrafted; if it plainly needs counsel, heading 6 says so and the Read stops.

**The follow-up rule.** Two readings. The Brand Strategist's: *none unless they write
back.* The brief's, as the CEO reads it: *one touch.* **The brief is followed:** a
delivered Read is followed by exactly one touch — the day-30 breakdown, with no reference
to the Read and no ask — then nothing unless they write. Touch five is never sent to
someone who took a Read. Nothing is asked in return, not even "did it help".

## 4. Cadence and limits

**Starts.** **Five sequences a week**, Tuesday–Thursday mornings, for six weeks (14 Sep –
23 Oct): thirty in sequence by the end of week six, ten spare names; after that, a start
only replaces a sequence that closed. The roadmap's ten a week would exhaust thirty
names in three weeks while a sequence runs six. The brief's ten a week are *conversations* — a reply in their own words, a call, a coffee, a
Read delivered — the weekly target on the sheet.

**Ceilings.** Twenty-five outbound touches a week; three Reads a week at most, each a
selling hour. Zero replies across thirty started sequences → rewrite touches two and
three before another touch five goes out (Analytics §2). The 60-day review (week of
2026-11-06) asks Analytics §2's four questions, nothing else.

**A no is a no for six months.** Any *no, not now, we have people, remove me* holds the
person 183 days: no touch, no connection request, no comment aimed at
them; their public events are still read. After the hold, one Warm reconnect with
something useful — never a restarted sequence. A second no closes the person. Silence
is not a no.

**What is logged, on tables that exist.**

| Fact | Where, today |
|---|---|
| A named person | `decision_makers`: `name`, `title`, `linkedin`, `role_category` (the seat), `source_urls_json` (the public credit; empty disqualifies), `last_verified`, `agency_id`. |
| Each touch | `agency_outreach`: `kind` = `linkedin` / `email` / `call`, `direction` = `out`, `contact` = the name as in `decision_makers`, `occurred_at` = send date, `note` begins `t1`…`t5`. |
| A reply · a Read · a no · a referral out | `agency_outreach`, `direction` = `in` (`responded` = 1 on a reply); `note` begins `reply · {seat}`, `read-requested` / `read-delivered`, `no · {reason} · hold-until YYYY-MM-DD`, or `referred-to · {who}`. The Read rows feed the sheet's two Read columns; the hold date is read before any touch. |
| A conversation becomes a job | An `opportunities` row: `source` = `outbound` (Analytics §3), `contact_name` / `contact_role` / `contact_linkedin`, `agency_id`; touches from here on `outreach_events` (Email / Call / LinkedIn / Meeting; Sent / Received / Note), which stamps `last_contacted`. |
| Win / loss | `opportunities.status` → `Won` / `Lost` / `Passed`; the first line of `notes` is `Lost: {reason}` / `Passed: {reason}`. Without exception, the day it arrives. |

**What is missing**, by Analytics §5 item: **1** the Read as a record; **3** the seat on
a reply (`contact` is free text; `role_category` already classifies it); **4** a required
reason on `Lost` / `Passed`; **5** the touch position and the hold date; **7** the
referrer as a person. The note prefixes above are the stopgap until the founder rules on
freeze exceptions (brief §13.3).

## 5. The six objections, in the founder's voice

The playbook's Stage 5 lists its objections in rank order; these are the first six, each
under eighty words, nothing the room, the package or the certificate cannot show.

**"Too expensive."**
> Fair. A library or a single writer prices one person's writing time. My number has two
> parts, both on the page: what it costs to make the piece, and what the use is worth —
> media, territory, term, exclusivity. If the band you have is real, I'll show you the
> scope that fits it, in writing, or tell you it's not this one. What I won't do is quote
> your budget back to you as the price.

**"The other house quotes one number."**
> Their number has a licence inside it too; it just isn't shown. Mine is split so you can
> see which lines you control. Term, territory and exclusivity move the licence fee, and
> you can move them. Same total, two parts, each with its reasons — so when your head of
> production asks why, the answer is on the page and not in my head.

**"You're in a weird middle."**
> I am. On a first job that's deliberate: small enough that if I miss, it's survivable
> for you; real enough that if I don't, it means something. Judge it on the whole
> delivery — the music, the stems, the cutdowns, the cue sheet, the grant of rights — and
> put me in whichever drawer it earns.

**"We need it in a week." / "The edit isn't locked."**
> Give me the delivery date, not the air date; I build from the first. Send the cut when
> you have it, with its frame rate and start timecode — a new cut is parked until I've
> stated the offset, so every note moves with the picture. If the calendar can't hold
> work I'd sign the certificate for, I'll say so before the deposit, not after.

**"We have people for this."**
> Keep them — on the flagship you should. I'm asking for the job they're too busy or too
> expensive for, and to be judged on the whole delivery next to theirs: music, stems,
> cutdowns, cue sheet, the rights paper. I won't say a word against them. If they're
> good, you'll have two.

**"Why not just use a generator?" / "Your no-AI line is unenforceable."**
> Two answers. Machine output has no author, so there's nothing to own and nothing to
> defend — the Copyright Office and a federal appeals court said so last year. And you're
> right: nobody can watch a composer's laptop, me included. What I can show you is that
> the agreement says we don't generate audio, every composer signs a standing agreement
> that says the same, and the name on the cue sheet is a person's.

- **The outreach engine's three drafts and its next-best-action** (`outreach_engine.py`):
  touch one asks for a call, the value line carries two banned phrases (Brand Strategist
  §7.14), and the cadence knows neither the sequence nor the six-month hold. The five
  texts replace them; the log tables stay. No code touched.
- **Ten new sequences a week** → five. Thirty names over six weeks.
- **Email as touch one.** A connection request about their work cannot read as a
  solicitation.
- **The first-touch page as a "bonus link"** (playbook Stage 1.1), and **any player,
  recording or site link** in a touch until brief §8 is done.
- **"Music your legal team can defend"** in the sequence. Business affairs only.
- **Business affairs as a cold target.** Sequenced only after their producer replies.
- **A Read on our own paper; a Read form; a question after a Read.** Spec by another
  door; a funnel and a freeze breach; the pitch the Strategist warned of.
- **"A renewal price fixed now"; "the music is the easy part"** — in the playbook's
  objection answers. Not shipped; banned.
- **Cold calls, gifts, a second sequence for non-responders.** Silence is Cold.
- **The "hundred", the 40/25/15, the six archetypes.** Thirty named people, sixty days.
