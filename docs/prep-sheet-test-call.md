# The prep sheet test call — Fen & Foundry

A test for **the sheet**, not for the extractor. The previous two scripts were monologues:
one person reciting a brief, which measures whether the engine can read. This one measures
whether you asked.

## The one rule

**The client answers only what is asked, and volunteers nothing.**

That is the whole design. If you never ask about territory, the word never enters the
transcript, and the gap afterwards is not the engine's fault or the client's. It is a line
of the question bank that failed. That is the only thing this call is testing.

## How to run it

Open the prep sheet (**📋 Call prep** on the meeting card) and the answer key side by side,
with the notetaker on. Work down the sheet in your own words. After each question you ask,
find the matching answer below and read it aloud.

**Do not read ahead.** The answers are ordered by topic, not by the sheet, so you have to
look each one up — which means you can only read an answer to a question you actually asked.

Most answers have a **second half that only exists if you ask the follow-up.** That is
deliberate: the follow-ups are where this bank earns or loses its place, and half the real
money in this call is behind them.

It runs about eight minutes if you ask everything.

## The setup

You are on a call with **Marta Vance**, brand lead at **Fen & Foundry**, a coffee roaster.
No agency. They are going national in the spring.

Open the call however you normally do. She will not brief you. She will wait.

## The answer key

### Budget

**Asked:** "We've got about a hundred and ten thousand for the campaign."

> **Follow-up only** ("is that all-in including the licence, ceiling or target?"):
> "Oh — for *music*? Music is thirty-eight. Thirty-eight thousand, and that has to
> include the licence. The hundred and ten is the whole production."

### Timeline

**Asked:** "We go live March 4th."

> **Follow-up only** ("are those the same date, anything upstream still moving?"):
> "No, we need it two weeks before that, February 18th, because the stations need it for
> clearance. And the colour grade isn't locked. That's supposed to be February 2nd and it
> has already slipped twice."

### Deliverables

**Asked:** "The two-minute film, and a sixty and a thirty for TV."

> **Follow-up only** ("stems on all of them?"):
> "Yes, stems on everything. Oh — and there's one more. We need a fifteen-second loop for
> in-store. It plays on a screen behind the counter all day."

### Business objective

**Asked:** "We've been a regional thing for eleven years and we're going national in the
spring. This is the campaign that introduces us to people who have never heard of us."

> **Follow-up only** ("how will you know it worked?"):
> "Trial. We want people to buy a bag once. Awareness is nice, trial is the number."

### What the music must do

**Asked:** "There's a two-minute film about the roastery. The music needs to carry it."

> **Follow-up only** ("carrying it, or supporting a voiceover?"):
> "Carrying. We cut the voiceover in round three, it made it feel like an advert."

### Emotional arc

**Asked:** "Warm. Handmade. Not precious about it."

> **Follow-up only** ("is there a turn, or does it hold?"):
> "It builds. One instrument at the start, the whole room by the end. But it must never get
> sentimental. The last lot gave us something that sounded like an insurance advert."

### References

**Asked:** "Our founder keeps sending me Bon Iver."

> **Follow-up only** ("which part are you reacting to?"):
> "The texture, not the melancholy. And between us, I think Bon Iver is wrong for this. Too
> sad. Don't build toward it. What I actually like is early Punch Brothers, and some
> Andrew Bird."

### Who signs off

**Asked:** "Me, mostly."

> **Follow-up only** ("anyone else whose opinion changes the outcome?"):
> "Our founder, Ruth Ellery. She doesn't sit on calls, but nothing ships without her. She
> will see it once, at the end."

### The brand

**Asked:** "We're careful about sounding corporate. Eleven years of being small is our
whole story."

> **Follow-up only** ("has music let you down before?"):
> "Twice. Both times it was too polished."

### The agency / your side

**Asked:** "There isn't an agency. We do it in house."

> **Follow-up only** ("anything about your process I should plan around?"):
> "Our finance person is part time, Tuesdays and Thursdays. Anything needing a signature or
> a payment only moves on those two days."

### Licence term

**Asked:** "A year, I think?"

> **Follow-up only** ("from delivery, or from first air?"):
> "From first air. So March 4th."

### Renewal

**Asked:** "We'd want the option to keep going if it works."

> **Follow-up only** ("price agreed now, or at the time?"):
> "Now, if you can. I don't want a surprise in twelve months."

### Territory

**Asked:** "US."

> **Follow-up only** ("any chance it extends later?"):
> "Canada, next year, probably. We already ship there."

### Exclusivity

**Asked:** "I hadn't thought about it. What do people normally do?"

> **Follow-up only** ("for how long?"):
> "If it isn't expensive, category exclusivity for the term. I wouldn't want to hear it in a
> supermarket own-brand coffee advert."

### Publishing and ownership

**Asked:** "No idea. Is that something we should care about?"

> **Follow-up only** ("does your legal team have a position?"):
> "We don't really have a legal team. Our accountant looks at contracts."

### PRO registration

**Asked:** "I don't know what that is."

> **Follow-up only** ("who files it on your side?"):
> "Nobody. If it needs doing, you would have to tell us what to do."

### Payment terms

**Asked:** "We normally pay on invoice, thirty days."

> **Follow-up only** ("is there a deposit, what triggers the final invoice?"):
> "We can do a deposit. Half up front is fine. Final on delivery — but remember the Tuesday
> and Thursday thing."

### Musicians

**Asked:** "No requirement from us."

> **Follow-up only** ("does that come from you or from the client?"):
> "From us, and we don't have one. Although if it's going on national TV, is there something
> we should be doing? You would know better than me."

## What this is measuring

Six things are **only** obtainable through a follow-up. They are the ones worth the fee:

| Only a follow-up gets it | Cost of missing it |
|---|---|
| **Music budget is $38,000**, not the $110,000 campaign figure | You quote against a number 3× too big and lose the deal, or win it and can't staff it |
| **Delivery is February 18th**, not the March 4th air date | Two weeks of schedule you thought you had |
| **The colour grade may slip** from February 2nd | The dependency that eats the two weeks |
| **A fifteen-second in-store loop** exists | An undelivered asset, and an in-store usage right nobody licensed |
| **Ruth Ellery** approves, and sees it once | One review from someone who has seen no drafts |
| **Bon Iver is wrong**, Punch Brothers is right | You build toward the reference the client is trying to kill |

And two consistency checks, because the same fact appears twice from different questions:

- the **Tuesday / Thursday** finance constraint (from *the agency* and from *payment terms*)
- the **term running from first air, March 4th** (from *licence term* and from *timeline*)

## Scoring it

Afterwards, open the Campaign Brief and check three things in this order.

**1. The canonical slots.** Budget must read **$38,000**. If it reads $110,000, the
follow-up was skipped and the sheet failed at its single most expensive line. Timeline must
carry **both** dates. Deliverables must have **four** items.

**2. The open questions.** Before this build, a call like this produced fourteen. Count what
comes back now. Every one of them maps to a line of the sheet you did not reach — that is
the list of questions to rewrite, not a list of client follow-ups to email.

**3. Your own read.** Which questions felt natural, and which felt like reading off a form?
The bank is written text in `call_prep.py` and changing it is one edit. A question that
makes a call worse is worth deleting even if it protects something.

The bar is not "did the engine capture it". This call is not hard to extract from. The bar
is **how much of it existed to be captured** — and that is entirely a function of what you
asked.
