# Role-play brief — you are Nadia Okonjo, The Larkspur Trust

**What this is.** Jon is testing whether his software captures a conversation properly.
You're playing the client. He'll run a normal discovery call and ask you questions; you
answer from the sheet below. Afterwards the software writes a summary and he checks it
against what you actually said.

**It takes about fifteen minutes.** You need this page and a Zoom link. Nothing else.

---

## The one rule that makes this work

> **Answer only what he asks. Volunteer nothing.**

That's the entire test. If he never asks where the film will be shown, you never say it,
and afterwards that gap tells him his question list has a hole in it. If you help him out
by mentioning things he didn't ask about, the test measures nothing.

It will feel unnatural. Real clients do it constantly.

**Second rule:** most answers below have a **second part in a grey box.** Only read that
part if he asks the follow-up question printed above it. If he asks the first question and
moves on, he doesn't get it. That's the point — the follow-ups are what's being tested.

---

## Who you are

You're **Nadia Okonjo**, Head of Fundraising at **The Larkspur Trust**, a wildlife charity.
You've worked there six years. You're warm, a bit rushed, and not a music person — if he
uses jargon, it's fine to say "sorry, what does that mean?"

You're making a **three-minute film** for the winter appeal. It's the biggest fundraising
push of your year.

**Don't open with any of that.** Let him ask. If he starts with small talk, be friendly and
brief, then wait.

---

## The answer key

Find the topic he's asking about. Read the answer aloud in your own words — you don't have
to say it exactly as written.

---

### If he asks who's on the call / who you are

"I'm Nadia, I run fundraising here. It's just me today."

> **Only if he asks who isn't here who has an opinion:**
> "Our Director of Conservation, Peter. He won't come to a call about music but he'll
> absolutely have thoughts when he sees it."

### If he asks about recording / a notetaker

"Yes, that's fine."

*(If he doesn't ask at all, say nothing — that's a miss worth knowing about.)*

### If he asks what the campaign is trying to do

"It's our winter appeal. Last year we raised about four hundred thousand and this year we
need to beat that, because we've got a reserve purchase going through in March that
depends on it."

> **Only if he asks how you'll know it worked:**
> "Donations in the first ten days. That's the number the board looks at. Everything else
> is noise to them."

### If he asks what the music has to do in the film

"Carry it, I think. There's a voiceover — one of our rangers — but there's a long section
in the middle with no words at all, just footage."

> **Only if he asks about that middle section specifically:**
> "It's about ninety seconds. That's the bit that has to do the work. If people are going
> to give, they decide there."

### If he asks what you need delivered

"The three-minute film, and a thirty-second version for social."

> **Only if he asks about stems, or 'anything else':**
> "Oh — and we're screening it at a fundraising dinner in February, on a big screen with a
> proper sound system. Does that need something different? And yes, whatever stems are, our
> video person asked for them."

### If he asks about the sound / how it should feel

"Hopeful. Not sad. We've done sad and it doesn't work any more — people switch off."

> **Only if he asks whether it holds one feeling or changes:**
> "It should start quite still and open up. But it mustn't get grand. There's a version of
> this that sounds like a nature documentary trailer and I'd hate that."

### If he asks about references / what you've been listening to

"Somebody on the team sent me a big charity advert from a couple of years ago, the one with
the piano."

> **Only if he asks which part you're reacting to, or whether it's the right direction:**
> "Honestly? I don't like it. It's manipulative. I'd rather something plainer — there's a
> piece of music in a documentary I saw, just a cello and a lot of space. That's closer."

### If he asks about timing

"The appeal launches November 3rd."

> **Only if he asks when you need it delivered, or what's upstream:**
> "We'd need it two weeks before, so mid-October, because it goes through our media agency
> for the paid placements. And the edit isn't locked — our filmmaker is still waiting on
> footage from a shoot that keeps getting rained off."

### If he asks about budget

"The appeal budget is ninety thousand."

> **Only if he asks what's allocated to music specifically:**
> "Ah — for the music, six thousand. And I'm afraid that's it. It's a grant-funded line and
> I can't move it. It'd need to cover everything, including whatever the licensing costs."

### If he asks who approves it

"Me, day to day."

> **Only if he asks whether anyone else's opinion changes the outcome:**
> "The board of trustees signs off anything public-facing. They meet quarterly. The next one
> is October 8th — if we miss that, the next is January, which is after the appeal."

### If he asks about the organisation / how you show up

"We're small. Nine people. We're careful about not looking like we spend donors' money on
slick things."

> **Only if he asks whether music has let you down before:**
> "We had a piece done for free two years ago by a supporter. It was lovely and completely
> wrong for the film, and nobody wanted to say so."

### If he asks how your side works / who moves paperwork

"That's me as well, mostly."

> **Only if he asks about anything to plan around:**
> "Our finance officer is only in on Wednesdays. And anything over five thousand needs two
> signatures, so it's never quick."

### If he asks how long you need to use the music

"Forever, ideally?"

> **Only if he asks from when, or whether you mean perpetual:**
> "I mean I don't want to have to come back and pay again every year. If it's a few years
> with an option, that's probably fine — I just can't have it expire in the middle of an
> appeal."

### If he asks about renewal

"I hadn't thought about it as a separate thing."

> **Only if he asks whether you'd want the price agreed now:**
> "Yes, please. I can't take a surprise number to the board."

### If he asks where it will run / territory

"UK, mostly."

> **Only if he asks if it might extend:**
> "It's on our YouTube, so I suppose anywhere? I don't know if that counts."

### If he asks about exclusivity

"What does that mean?"

*(Let him explain. Then:)* "I wouldn't want to hear it in another charity's advert, no."

> **Only if he asks for how long:**
> "However long we're using it, I suppose. Is that expensive?"

### If he asks about publishing or ownership

"No idea. Is that something I need to worry about?"

> **Only if he asks whether you have legal support:**
> "We use a solicitor who does pro bono work for us. She's very good but she's slow because
> she's doing it for free."

### If he asks about PRO registration or cue sheets

"I don't know what that is."

> **Only if he asks who'd handle it:**
> "Nobody. You'd have to tell us what to do."

### If he asks about payment

"We pay on invoice, thirty days."

> **Only if he asks about a deposit or what triggers the final invoice:**
> "We can't do a deposit, I'm afraid — the grant pays on delivery. That's a condition of
> the funding."

### If he asks about musicians / union

"No requirement from us."

> **Only if he asks whether that's your rule or someone else's:**
> "Actually — could we use children? There's a primary school we work with and their choir
> is lovely. The kids would be thrilled. Is that complicated?"

### If he reads the summary back to you at the end

Listen. **If he gets the budget wrong — if he says ninety thousand instead of six — let him
say it, then correct him.** Note whether he read it back at all.

### If he asks what he hasn't asked about

"You haven't asked whether we've got any footage of the reserve yet. We haven't. The whole
film might change if the shoot doesn't happen."

*(This is important and he has no way to reach it except by asking. If he never asks,
never say it.)*

### If he asks who else should get the summary

"Send it to me, and copy Peter — our Director of Conservation. He'll want to see it even
though he won't reply."

## Afterwards — the three things to tell Jon

You don't need to know anything about the software. Just these:

1. **Did he ask you to correct the summary at the end?** Yes or no.
2. **Which of the grey boxes did you never get to read?** Those are the follow-ups he
   didn't ask. Just list the topics.
3. **How did it feel?** Rushed, natural, like a form being filled in? That's the part no
   test can measure, and it's the most useful thing you'll tell him.

Thank you. Genuinely — this is the only way to find out whether the thing works.
