# Front-of-House Design — Executive Council Deliberation

**Convened:** 2026-06-17 · **Chair:** Jon Shipp (CEO)
**Input:** `CHORDENTIAL_LANDING_PAGE_REVISED_2.19.pdf` (13-section "magazine / case-led
experience" framework) and the two brand wordmarks (knockout/white + dark).
**Mandate:** decide what the public site looks like, where the logo goes, and what
we will and won't ship — using the PDF as guidance, not gospel.

This was **not** a rubber-stamp. The council disagreed on three substantive points
and the CEO ruled on each. Decisions are marked **[RATIFIED]** or **[OVERRIDE]**
where we departed from the PDF.

---

## 1. Overall aesthetic — dark-cinematic vs. warm-editorial

**Brand Strategist:** The PDF is right. Agency creative directors and producers are
our buyers; they live in dark, cinematic, confident work. A full-bleed scoring-session
hero with a serif headline reads as "working studio," not "concept." Go dark.

**Creative Director (dissent):** Careful — "dark cinematic agency site" is the most
copied look in the industry. Every post house and audio shop has the moody console
shot. Our actual brand equity is the *warm* palette — cream, sand, wine, that orange
chord mark. A generic dark site throws away the one thing that's ownable.

**CMO:** Both are right, and the answer isn't either/or. The PDF itself is a *magazine* —
magazines open with a cinematic cover and then set editorial body copy on warm paper.

**[RATIFIED] Hybrid:** Cinematic **dark** hero and section breaks (authority, "working
company"); **warm editorial** body sections (cream/sand paper, wine headings) for the
thesis, process, and delivery copy. The orange chord mark is the connective accent
throughout. This is cinematic *and* ownable — not generic-dark.

## 2. Fabricated trust logos & testimonials — the integrity fight

**CMO:** The PDF's "Trusted by teams in… H&M / Vanguard / FedEx" strip and the named
testimonials are the highest-converting elements. Buyers need social proof.

**CFO (hard objection):** We have **no** such clients. Putting H&M and FedEx logos on a
live site is fabricated social proof — legally exposed (trademark/false association)
and a direct violation of the company's honesty principle (win/loss truth is the
moat; we do **not** invent it). I will not sign off on fake logos.

**Founder's Advocate:** Concur, and it's strategically dumber than it looks. The first
real agency buyer who asks "you worked with FedEx?" and hears "no" — we've burned the
exact trust this page exists to build.

**CMO (revised):** Then give me *honest* confidence signals instead of nothing.

**[OVERRIDE — departs from the PDF] No fabricated proof.**
- **No** real-brand client logos. Replace the trust strip with honest **category**
  framing: "Built for teams in Retail · Financial · Healthcare · National brands"
  (the *kind* of work we're built for, not a claim of having done it).
- **No** invented testimonials. The testimonials section ships as an honest
  **"Early pilots"** frame; real quotes drop in as they exist. Any sample copy is
  visibly labeled *illustrative*.
- **Featured Work** case cards are clearly marked **Sample** until real campaigns
  exist. We show the *format* of proof, not fake proof.

This is the one place we firmly overrule the draft. Everything else in the PDF is
honest positioning; this part is borrowed credibility we haven't earned yet.

## 3. Logo placement & which mark

**Creative Director:** Two marks: the **knockout/white** wordmark for dark surfaces,
the **dark wine** wordmark for light surfaces. Use them correctly or the orange
chord mark disappears (on white the KO is invisible; on near-black the dark mark is).

**Brand Strategist:** Header should be quiet — wordmark **top-left**, small, so the
hero image carries. A centered logo competes with the headline.

**CTO:** Whatever we pick must be system fonts + the existing PNGs — no heavy webfont
on the free tier.

**[RATIFIED]:**
- **Header (dark):** knockout/white wordmark, **top-left**, small. Nav right.
- **Light editorial sections / footer:** dark wine wordmark.
- **One large brand moment:** the chord mark + wordmark centered in the closing CTA
  band (a single editorial "signature," not repeated chrome).
- **Display type:** system **serif** (Georgia stack) for headlines — the PDF's
  editorial authority, zero webfont cost. **Inter** stays for body.

## 4. How much to build now (no empty editorial)

**COO (objection):** The PDF has 10 sections including Field Notes (articles),
testimonials, and multiple case studies. We have *none* of that content. A magazine
with empty pages looks worse than a tight one-pager.

**CMO:** Agreed — build the **spine** honestly, stub the rest with intent.

**[RATIFIED] Ship now:** Hero · honest category strip · the operational-failure thesis
("Music rarely fails creatively. It fails operationally.") · the 4-step Approach
(Direction Lock → Structural Composition → Controlled Variation → Bounded Delivery) ·
"What delivery actually looks like" (operational clarity — this is *true* today via
the estimator/process) · honest Featured-Work (sample-labeled) · About · closing
confidence CTA. **Stub with honest labels:** Field Notes (one "coming" teaser),
testimonials (pilot frame). Pricing stays *outcome-led*, not price-first (per PDF §8).

## 5. Copy the council kept from the PDF [RATIFIED]
- Headline: **"Music that moves campaigns forward — and gets approved."**
- Thesis: **"Music rarely fails creatively. It fails operationally."**
- Close: **"See how your next campaign could sound."**
- Dual CTA: **View Work** (to the work) + **Start a conversation** (to intake).
- Positioning: clarity · control · confidence in delivery; contained revisions;
  approval-ready for multi-stakeholder sign-off. *This is the whole pitch.*
