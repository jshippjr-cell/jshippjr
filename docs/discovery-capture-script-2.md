# The capture script — second call

A discovery call to read aloud, written to break the parts of the pipeline the **first**
script could not reach.

Script one (`discovery-comprehension-script.md`) tested whether the engine could *read* a
hard brief. It passed, and the product still showed an empty Budget field, because reading
and **filing** are different jobs and only one of them was under test. Script one also
exercised four of the ten canonical slots, so a failure in the other six could not have
shown up.

This one is built the other way round. Every one of the **ten canonical fields** is stated
somewhere in it, so the answer key is a checklist of slots rather than a list of facts, and
"the engine got it" is not a passing grade unless the field on the page holds it.

Different brand, different genre, different numbers from script one on purpose: a run that
passes by recognising the last script is not a test.

Read it into a Zoom call with the notetaker on, at a normal pace, pausing at the paragraph
breaks. It runs about four minutes.

---

## The script

> Hey, thanks for the time. So, quick context. We're Fieldhouse, we're the agency of record
> for Halden, the outdoor apparel brand, and this is for their fortieth anniversary. The big
> picture for them is that they've aged with their customer and they need to matter to people
> under thirty-five again. That's the whole reason this exists.
>
> What we're making is a six-minute documentary short. The music's job in it is narrower than
> that: carry the film, and make forty years feel like one continuous thing rather than a
> retrospective.
>
> On what we need delivered: the six-minute master, a two-minute cut, a sixty and a thirty
> for broadcast, nine-by-sixteen and one-by-one for social, and stems on all of it. We are
> not doing a radio version. Somebody always asks, the answer is no.
>
> Budget. I'll be direct with you. What I've got approved is between fifty-five and seventy
> thousand. Sorry, fifty-five to sixty-five. Seventy was the old number before they cut it.
> Sixty-five is a hard ceiling, that's US dollars, and it's inclusive of the license. For
> context, total production on the film is about nine hundred thousand, and there's a
> separate two hundred K media buy behind it, so nobody's staring at the music line. But I
> can't go back for more.
>
> Timing has two dates in it and they're not the same date. The anniversary is October third
> and that's immovable, that's when it airs. But broadcast QC needs final delivery three
> weeks ahead of that, so we're really talking mid-September. And the wrinkle is the edit
> lock keeps moving. Right now it's August twenty-second. If that slips a week, everything
> after it slips with it.
>
> Tonally, it should start almost silent. One instrument. It needs to feel restrained the
> whole way through, then there's one moment of lift about four minutes in when the archive
> footage comes in, and then it pulls back again. It must not become triumphant. The
> temptation is triumphant. Resist it.
>
> For references, think Jóhann Jóhannsson, and some of the quieter Hildur Guðnadóttir work.
> The client keeps sending me Explosions in the Sky. That's the wrong energy and I'm going to
> talk them out of it, so don't build toward that.
>
> On process: I'm your day-to-day. Priya runs the account and she'll be on every call, but
> she doesn't sign anything. Final approval is Halden's brand director, Tom Vasquez, and
> realistically he sees it once. So we get one shot with him.
>
> Two things I want to flag. First, there's archive footage in the film from a 1986 broadcast
> and we do not have the audio rights to what's underneath it, so whatever you write has to
> work as a full replacement for that section, not sit alongside it. Second, Fieldhouse is
> slow on paper. Anything needing a signature should start moving early.
>
> And we'd want a check-in every Tuesday, fifteen minutes, and everything in writing
> afterwards. Tom's team won't act on a verbal.
>
> That's it. Enough to work with?

## The answer key

Score in **two columns**, because the two failures this product has actually had were
different failures:

1. **Did the engine read it?** Open **View transcripts & evidence**. The right-hand column
   is what was extracted.
2. **Did it land in the field?** Look at Campaign Intelligence itself. A fact that is only
   in the evidence column is a **routing failure**, not a reading failure, and it is the one
   that has bitten twice — everything downstream (the estimate, the brief, the proposal)
   reads the fields, not the evidence.

A row is only green when **both** are.

### The ten canonical slots

| Field | Expected | Why it's in the script |
|---|---|---|
| **Business objective** | Matter to under-35s again for the 40th anniversary | Deliberately stated *separately* from the campaign objective, one paragraph apart |
| **Campaign objective** | Carry a 6-minute doc; make 40 years feel continuous | The pair above is the test: two objectives, two slots, one must not swallow the other |
| **Budget** | **$55,000–$65,000 USD**, hard ceiling, license included | Range + spoken correction + hard cap + currency + two distractor numbers |
| **Timeline** | Air **Oct 3** (immovable); final delivery ~3 weeks prior (**mid-Sept**); contingent on an **Aug 22** edit lock that is slipping | Two dates that are not the same date, plus a live dependency |
| **Deliverables** | 6-min master, 2-min cut, :60, :30, 9:16, 1:1, stems on all | Seven items in one breath, mixed spoken and written forms |
| **Decision makers** | **Tom Vasquez, Halden brand director** — final approval, sees it once | Two other named humans who are *not* the approver |
| **Brand notes** | Halden: 40 years old, aged with its customer, outdoor apparel | Brand and agency are different parties and have different slots |
| **Agency notes** | Fieldhouse: agency of record, **slow on paper / signatures** | The one operational fact about the agency, buried in a risk paragraph |
| **Emotional arc** | Near-silent open, one instrument, restraint, single lift ~4:00, then pull back | Long, sequential, and ends in a prohibition |
| **Reference playlist** | Jóhann Jóhannsson; Hildur Guðnadóttir (quieter work) | With a third artist attached and then withdrawn |

### The traps

1. **The correction.** "fifty-five to seventy. Sorry, fifty-five to sixty-five." Taking the
   first range over-quotes by $5,000 at the top. Script one had a single corrected figure;
   this is a corrected **range**, which is harder.

2. **Two distractors, different sizes.** $900,000 is *production*; $200,000 is *media*.
   Either one in the Budget field is a number that walks into a proposal.

3. **The ceiling.** "Sixty-five is a hard ceiling." A range without that is a range you can
   negotiate upward, and this one you cannot. If it is captured only as `$55k–$65k` with no
   note of the cap, mark it **partial**.

4. **Currency, stated.** Script one produced an open question asking to confirm the currency.
   Here it is said out loud — "that's US dollars" — so a currency question this time is a
   read failure, not diligence.

5. **Two dates that are not the same date.** Air date and delivery date. A single "October 3"
   in Timeline is **wrong**: it books three weeks of schedule that do not exist.

6. **The moving dependency.** The Aug 22 edit lock is not a fact about the campaign, it is a
   fact about *your* schedule. It should surface as a dependency or a risk.

7. **The negation, twice.** "We are not doing a radio version" and "It must not become
   triumphant." Present as requirements = failure. Recorded as exclusions = pass.

8. **The disavowed reference.** Explosions in the Sky is named and then argued against by the
   speaker. Listing it beside Jóhannsson loses the speaker's actual judgement.

9. **Three people, three roles.** Speaker = day-to-day. Priya = present, no authority.
   Tom Vasquez = signs. A read that flattens them cannot tell you whose approval to chase.

10. **Brand vs agency.** Halden facts and Fieldhouse facts must not pool into one field.
    They are two slots because they are two organisations (ADR-0056).

11. **The rights constraint.** The 1986 archive audio cannot be licensed, so the cue must
    *replace* it. That is a scope constraint disguised as background.

12. **No slot exists for these.** "Tuesday, fifteen minutes" and "everything in writing, Tom's
    team won't act on a verbal." Neither is a canonical field. Both are real. They must land
    as dynamic fields rather than be dropped — that is the whole reason the intelligence
    stopped being a fixed form.

## After the call, check the chain

The point of the canonical slots is that other things read them. Two checks worth thirty
seconds each, because they are where a routing failure actually costs money:

- **The estimate.** Open it. If Budget filled, the estimate should be working against
  $55k–$65k. If it is working against nothing, or against $900,000, the slot did not fill
  no matter what the evidence page shows.
- **The brief.** Deliverables and Timeline should read back as the seven assets and the
  mid-September delivery. A brief that says "October 3" is a brief that has lost three weeks.

## How to score it

- **Free keyword pass** (no API credit, badge reads `baseline`): expect the numbers and some
  of the deliverables. It cannot do negation, attribution, or two-dates-one-meaning, and it
  does not claim to.
- **Ten-agent engine**: expect all ten slots populated and at least traps 1, 2, 5, 7 and 8
  handled. Anything **confidently wrong** — a date, a budget, a vocal requirement that was
  refused — is a bug worth sending me, not a tuning preference.

The bar is unchanged from script one, and it is not "how many facts": **would I put a number
in front of this client based on what the page says?** A field that is empty asks you a
question. A field that is wrong does not.
