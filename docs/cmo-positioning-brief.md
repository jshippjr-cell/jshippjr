# Chordential — CMO Positioning Brief (v1)

*Author: CMO. First work product under the CMO charter. Takes positions; the CEO
can override. Grounded in the ratified strategy (A→B→C, lean, qualification-first)
and the company-definition round.*

---

## 1. Positioning — what category are we in?

**Decision: we lead with _Music Opportunity Intelligence_, positioned toward _the
operating system for commercial music procurement._**

- **The category we own now (the wedge):** *Music Opportunity Intelligence.*
  Specific, ownable, and literally true today — we find, qualify, estimate, and
  prepare commercial-music opportunities. No one else owns this phrase.
- **The category we are building toward (the vision):** *the operating system for
  commercial music procurement* (the Founder's Advocate north star). We grow into
  it; we don't claim it before the buyer-graph exists.

**Categories we explicitly reject, and why:**

| Tempting label | Why the CMO vetoes it |
|---|---|
| **"RFP platform"** | Commodity, race-to-the-bottom, implies gov bids and procurement bureaucracy. Anyone can poll RFPs. Dilutes us into a feature. |
| **"Creative intelligence platform"** | Vague, un-ownable, sounds like ad-tech. Nobody writes a check for "creative intelligence." |
| **"AI music tool"** | We do **not** generate music. Positioning us near Suno/Udio invites the wrong comparison and devalues the human craft we exist to serve. |
| **"Lead-gen / scraper"** | Sells volume; our value is *curation and qualification*, the opposite of volume. |

> **One-line positioning:** *Chordential is music opportunity intelligence — it
> finds the commercial music work worth winning, qualifies it, and tells you what
> to charge, before your competitors even see it.*

## 2. Buyer — who writes the check?

The **user** is rarely the **economic buyer**. The CMO separates them:

| ICP | User (operates it) | Economic buyer (writes the check) | Budget authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boutique/mid studio | Producer / composer | **Studio owner / principal** | Owns P&L |
| Production company | Music dept / producer | **Executive Producer** | Owns project budgets |
| Creative agency | Creative / producer | **Agency owner or Creative Director** | Owns client budgets |
| Brand / in-house | Brand/marketing manager | **Brand Marketing Director** | Owns campaign spend |
| Music supervision | Music supervisor | **Music Supervisor (often the buyer too)** | Owns music budget |

**Implication for messaging:** the *user* feels the pain (missed/late
opportunities); the *buyer* feels the **money** (lost revenue, wasted hours, bad
quotes). Sales copy speaks to the buyer's P&L; product copy speaks to the user's
workflow.

## 3. Problem — what *expensive* problem are we solving?

**Bad answer:** "Finding opportunities."

**Chordential's answer:**
> *We reduce the time and judgment required to identify, qualify, and price
> revenue-generating music opportunities — so winnable work stops slipping away to
> slower competitors, and nobody wastes a day quoting a job they'll never win.*

The three expensive sub-problems, in the buyer's language:

1. **Lost revenue** — "We found out too late, so we didn't bid." (winnable deals walk)
2. **Wasted senior time** — "My best people spend hours triaging junk leads and
   writing quotes for work we don't get." (qualification + estimation is manual)
3. **Mispricing** — "We guessed the budget and left money on the table — or lost
   on price." (no estimation confidence)

These are **payroll-and-revenue problems**, not "I wish I had more leads."

## 4. Differentiation — the forced answer

The CMO's job is to make the difference undeniable:

| "Why not just use…" | What it does | Why it loses to Chordential |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Alerts** | Keyword pings on the open web | No qualification, no music-craft judgment, no estimate, no buyer-fit, no pursue/pass. You still do 100% of the triage. |
| **A virtual assistant** | A human scans boards | Slow, inconsistent, doesn't price the work, doesn't learn, costs $1–3k/mo, and quits. No compounding data. |
| **GovWin** | Government RFP database | Gov-only, procurement-heavy, C-tier teaming work — the *least* Chordential-shaped music revenue. Wrong buyers. |
| **LinkedIn / job boards** | People post gigs | Unstructured, unqualified, no estimation, drowned in noise; you find out when everyone else does. |
| **Music libraries (Artlist, etc.)** | Sell existing tracks | Orthogonal — they sell *product*; we source *original-craft commissions*. |
| **AI music (Suno/Udio)** | Generate tracks | We don't compete with creation; we feed the humans who create. Different category entirely. |
| **Creative marketplaces (Upwork/SoundBetter)** | Match freelancers | Race-to-bottom labor matching; no opportunity intelligence, no curation, no estimation, no buyer graph. |

**The defensible difference:** Chordential is the only one that **qualifies music
craft, prices the job, and learns from every outcome.** Alerts and VAs give you
*more*; Chordential gives you *the right ones, priced, ranked, and explained.*

## 5. ICP profiles (v1)

Each ICP = pain · how they solve it today · willingness to pay · the one message.

**1. Agency Owner**
- *Pain:* winnable music briefs surface late; junior staff can't price music.
- *Today:* word of mouth + scattered inbound + gut-feel quotes.
- *WTP:* high — one won campaign dwarfs the subscription.
- *Message:* "Win the music work your agency is already qualified for — before the pitch closes."

**2. Executive Producer (production co.)**
- *Pain:* music is an afterthought; scrambles for composers and budgets late.
- *Today:* a rolodex + email; reactive.
- *WTP:* medium-high — protects project margin and timelines.
- *Message:* "Every project's music — sourced, scoped, and priced on day one."

**3. Creative Director (agency/brand)**
- *Pain:* needs the right sonic idea fast; can't tell craft from noise in inbound.
- *Today:* asks around; defaults to known vendors.
- *WTP:* medium — values speed and a curated shortlist.
- *Message:* "The qualified music partner shortlist, with budgets, in minutes."

**4. Music Supervisor**
- *Pain:* drowning in submissions; clearance and budget guesswork.
- *Today:* spreadsheets + relationships.
- *WTP:* medium — time is the scarce resource.
- *Message:* "Qualified, clearable, priced — stop triaging, start placing."

**5. Brand Manager (in-house creative)**
- *Pain:* doesn't know what music should cost or who to trust.
- *Today:* agency markup or random vendors.
- *WTP:* medium — wants confidence and cost control.
- *Message:* "Know what your campaign's music should cost — and who can deliver it."

## 6. Proposed feature — the Strategic-Value lens

The CMO's first build request. Today the app has two numbers: **alignment** (fit)
and **opportunity score** (attractiveness). Neither captures *strategic* value —
so a $10k one-off municipal bid currently outranks a $2k brief from a major agency
that could become a repeat enterprise account. **That is a positioning failure the
CMO must correct.**

**Strategic Value** = a third, CMO-owned signal (0–100), composed of three inputs
Jon specified:

1. **Buyer-type desirability** — *is this a Chordential customer?*
   | Buyer type | Weight |
   |---|---|
   | Agency | 1.0 |
   | Production company | 0.9 |
   | Brand | 0.85 |
   | Educational | 0.4 |
   | Government | 0.3 |

2. **Buyer relationship value** — *is the buyer valuable over time?*
   | Relationship | Weight |
   |---|---|
   | Enterprise / multi-project buyer | 1.0 |
   | Repeat buyer | 0.8 |
   | One-time project | 0.4 |

3. **Strategic importance** — *door-opener?* Marquee-brand / named-agency /
   foot-in-the-door multiplier even when the dollar value is small.

**The behavior that matters:** Strategic Value can **override raw budget** in
ranking and surface a callout —
> *"$2k now, but a door into [Major Agency] — strategically ranked above the $10k
> municipal one-off."*

This is presentation of a *marketing* judgment (which relationships compound),
layered on the existing engines — it does not change qualification or scoring
logic, it adds a complementary lens and a sort option.

**Open inputs needed from the data model:** buyer relationship (one-time / repeat /
enterprise) and a "marquee" flag aren't reliably known at intake yet — v1 infers
them (agency/brand name recognition, `Relationship` enum already in the model),
and the human can set them on the detail page (feeding the moat).
