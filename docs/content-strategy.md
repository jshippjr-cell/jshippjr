# Chordential — Content Strategy (v1)

*Author: Chief Content Strategist. Third work product of the Marketing Council, after the
Brand Strategy and the Messaging Framework, both of which this document inherits and does
not change. Dated 2026-09-08. The CEO can override any position taken here.*

> **The mission.** People should know Chordential long before they need Chordential. We
> earn that through education, transparency and expertise, never through advertising.
> This document designs the editorial system that makes Chordential the company agencies
> learn from. It does not contain content. It contains the machine that will make it.

> **The test this document is graded against.** *If Chordential disappeared tomorrow,
> would the industry miss one of its most valuable educational resources?* Section 8 turns
> that sentence into an audit that runs every quarter.

labelled as teaching campaigns. When the first client-approved case study exists, it slots
into a series that is already running.

What Chordential knows that its audience probably does not, organised by domain. Each row

### 1.1 How commercial music actually gets made

### 1.3 How approvals work

| What we know | Why the audience does not | Source | Own |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The client approves a direction, then a version, then a mix. Never "the music."** Approval is staged and cumulative; each stage narrows what may still change. | Buyers approve as if each approval were total, then discover what it froze. | §0.2, §3 | ★★★ |

| **De jure versus de facto approvers**: the presented-to person (agency producer) is rarely the real one (CD, or brand). Whether brand is in the room at the first presentation is the best predictor of late surprises. | Every agency has lived it; nobody has written it down as a rule. | §2 | ★★★ |

### 1.4 Revision management

| What we know | Why the audience does not | Source | Own |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A conform is not a revision.** A change caused by picture is a different species from a change caused by taste; only one is scope-bearing. Houses that do not distinguish them give away free work forever. | This is the single most valuable distinction in the domain and almost nobody outside a music house has the vocabulary. | §0.1, §8.4 | ★★★ |
| **The round is a countable unit** with contractual meaning: feedback, interpretation, response. The round clock starts at *consolidated* notes, not at the first of four conflicting emails. | Agencies count rounds informally and dispute them later. | §8.2, §10.5 | ★★★ |
| **The feedback dictionary**: "more energy" means tempo for one CD and arrangement density for another. Interpretation is the craft; getting it right in one round is what clients pay a house for. | Buyers think the note is clear. | §1 Phase D, §7 | ★★★ |
| **Defend or comply** is a three-way triage: take literally, interpret, or push back to protect the work. | Buyers experience pushback as friction, not as a service. | §10.4 | ★★★ |
| **Fidelity calibration**: how finished a version should sound at each stage. Too polished invites premature mix notes; too rough invites misjudgment. | Nobody tells the client why a demo sounds the way it does. | §10.6 | ★★★ |
| **Every note is priced before a creator sees it**: conform, revision, or out of scope. A note is not work until it is priced. | Buyers assume a note is free until they are told otherwise, late. | ADR-0069 (`docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE_DECISIONS.md`) | ★★★ |
| Market houses include **five revision rounds**; most freelancers include one or two. | Buyers do not know what is normal, so they cannot negotiate it. | `docs/market-pricing-research.md` §3 | ★★ |

| Unregistered cues **silently forfeit** the composer's income, which is a fairness issue on the supply side as much as a compliance one. | Buyers do not connect their paperwork to a composer's livelihood. | `production-lifecycle-model.md` §1 Phase G | ★★★ |

### 1.6 Rights ownership

| What we know | Why the audience does not | Source | Own |
|---|---|---|---|
| Work-for-hire versus licence-plus-splits; master versus publishing; "all-in" fees split roughly 50/50 under MFN parity; what a buyout actually transfers. | The vocabulary is legal and nobody translates it for producers. | `market-research.md` §3 | ★★ |
| **Chain of title** is the thing legal is actually checking: human-authored, assigned or WFH, no uncleared samples, no AI-only material. | Buyers ask "is it cleared?" without knowing what the question means. | `product-spec-clearance-certified.md` | ★★★ |

### 1.7 Licensing mistakes

| What we know | Why the audience does not | Source | Own |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The in-app library trap**: platform music libraries cover *native* posts only. ### 1.11 Music budgeting and what music should cost

| What we know | Why the audience does not | Source | Own |
|---|---|---|---|
| **There are two fees**: a creative fee (the cost of making it, at a target margin) and a licence fee (media × territory × term × exclusivity, capped). Most quotes hide one inside the other. | One-number quotes make like-for-like comparison impossible. | ADR-0065, `market-pricing-research.md` §1 | ★★★ |
| **The client's stated budget never sets the price.** It returns a verdict: below floor, in band, above band. | Buyers expect the quote to land on the budget and learn nothing from it. | ADR-0065 | ★★★ |
| **A 10× spread** between the cheapest and dearest bespoke commissions, driven by four levers: usage scope, musical complexity, rights model, timeline. | Buyers see the spread as arbitrary. | `market-pricing-research.md` §2 | ★★ |
| **The per-minute-rate trap**: freelance per-minute rates price one person's writing time. They do not price a producer, a mixer, a session, clearance, cue sheets, stems, delivery or the guarantee any of it arrives. Comparing a house to them is comparing a studio to a session player. | Every price-shopping buyer makes exactly this comparison. | `market-pricing-research.md` §3 | ★★★ |
| Market multipliers, cited: 3 years worldwide ≈ +80%; perpetual, worldwide, all media ≈ 2.5×; featured placement +50%; needle drop under 30s 0.5×. | The rate cards exist but nobody explains them to an advertiser. | `market-pricing-research.md` §4 | ★★ |
| Four multiplied factors **compound faster than the market moves**, which is why an honest model carries a ceiling. | Buyers receive a number that fell out of arithmetic nobody checked. | `market-pricing-research.md` §4 | ★★★ |
| Full-service agencies add **15–25%** on top of any production budget. | A brand paying a house directly should know what it is comparing. | `market-pricing-research.md` §2, §5 | ★★ |
| **Renewal is a revenue event with a date.** Term expiry and usage expansion are dated and priced. | Nobody owns the calendar after delivery. | `production-lifecycle-model.md` §1 Phase I | ★★★ |
| Fee anchors by medium, cited: local ad $1–25k; national TV $15k–250k+; library buyout $2–8k per track; music supervision 10–20% of the music budget. | Nobody publishes a plain map. | `market-research.md` §3 | ★★ |

### 1.16 What we do not know, and must not pretend to

The honesty rule applies to the knowledge inventory as much as to a client page.

- **No Chordential actuals yet.** Every pricing figure we publish is a market prior with a
  cited source, never "what we have found across our projects." When actuals exist, they
  are published as actuals, with the sample size.
- **No client-approved case study yet.** Until one exists, every worked example uses an
  invented brand and is labelled a teaching campaign.
- **We are not lawyers.** Rights and clearance content is educational, cites primary
  sources (Copyright Office, PRO documentation, published rate cards), and says so.
- **Craft is the composer's domain.** We teach how a house runs the room, not how to write
  a melody. Composition tutorials belong to composers; we credit them when we host them.
- **Union rates and procedures change.** Cite the current schedule and date it; never
  restate a number without its source and its date.
- **Rulings on AI and copyright are moving.** Track and explain each as it lands. Never
  predict a court.

## 2. Stage 2 — Content pillars

Seven long-term pillars. Each answers one major question the audience already asks, is
aimed at named ICPs from the positioning brief, and is fed by the inventory above. A piece
that maps to no pillar is out of scope until a pillar is amended, in the same way a feature
that maps to no stage of the mission spine is out of scope.

### Pillar 1 — The Business of Commercial Music
**The question it answers:** *What should music cost, where does the money go, and how do I
budget for it without guessing?*

- **Purpose.** Make the economics of commissioned and licensed music legible to the people
  who approve the budgets. Replace the one-number quote with an understanding of the levers.
- **Target audience.** Agency Owner (primary), Executive Producer, Brand Manager, agency
  business affairs and procurement.
- **Topics.** The two fees. The budget as a verdict. The four levers and the 10× spread.
  Term, media, territory and exclusivity, with cited multipliers. The per-minute-rate trap.
  Library economics versus commissioning. Agency overhead and direct-to-brand pricing.
  Renewal as a dated revenue event. Fee anchors by medium. How a young house estimates
  honestly before it has actuals. The honest market map (what the sync market is really
  worth, and why vendor figures bundle).
- **Why it builds authority.** Nobody in the market explains pricing with sources. Publishing
  the levers, the multipliers and the ceiling, with citations, makes Chordential the place a
  producer goes to check a quote from anyone. That is a reference position, not a sales one.
- **Evidence.** `market-pricing-research.md`, `market-research.md`, ADR-0065, the Hybrid
  Model Spec. The public `/pricing` page prices a worked example through the real model and
  is the one surface that may restate a number.
- **Honesty constraint.** Bands and levers, never quotes. Every number carries its source
  and date. No Chordential actuals until they exist; when they do, the sample size ships
  with them.

### Pillar 2 — Rights & Clearance
**The question it answers:** *Is this music actually safe to use, and how would I know?*

- **Purpose.** Teach what legal is really checking so that agencies can ask the right
  question of any vendor, including us.
- **Target audience.** Agency business affairs, brand legal, agency producers, Brand
  Managers running social-first campaigns, Music Supervisors.
- **Topics.** Chain of title. Work-for-hire versus licence-plus-splits; master versus
  publishing. What a licence's term, media and territory mean in practice. The in-app
  library trap. "Royalty-free" versus copyright-free. Content ID and safelisting. The
  soundalike problem. The sample audit. Cue sheets, PROs, ISRC and ISWC, and the black box.
  AI-only output and copyrightability, tracked ruling by ruling. What a clearance
  certificate contains. Usage past term and the silent renewal. Signatures bound to the
  document they sign.
- **Why it builds authority.** This is the pillar closest to the product the market pays
  for, and the one where the market is most under-served: the language is legal, the
  audience is not, and the failures are expensive and late. Owning the plain-English
  explanation of clearance makes "music your legal team can defend" a thing people
  learned from us before they heard it as a pitch.
- **Evidence.** `product-spec-clearance-certified.md`, `market-research.md` §3–4,
  `production-lifecycle-model.md` Phase G, ADR-0059, ADR-0082, primary sources (Copyright
  Office, PRO guidance, published rate cards).
- **Honesty constraint.** Educational, not legal advice, and it says so. Cite primary
  sources. "Documented, original, delivery-ready", never "litigation-proof".

### Pillar 3 — Behind the Campaign
**The question it answers:** *What actually happens between the brief and the air date?*

- **Purpose.** Make the whole production lifecycle visible, phase by phase, so that a
  buyer knows what they are approving, what it freezes, and what happens next.
- **Target audience.** Agency producers (primary), Creative Directors, Brand Managers new
  to commissioning music, Executive Producers at production companies.
- **Topics.** How to brief music (translation of a client brief into musician language;
  references and the reference-distance problem; what a brief must state and what a house
  will otherwise assume). The nine phases and their gates. The taste gate. Casting and the
  bake-off. Presentation as a staged event. Direction selection. Live versus programmed.
  Vocalist casting. The schedule computed backward. Picture lock as the master dependency.
  The version family and why the longest version comes first. What is different about a
  72-hour social sprint.
- **Why it builds authority.** The lifecycle map is the single most complete piece of
  domain knowledge Chordential holds, and it is the one buyers most want and least have.
  Publishing it serialised makes Chordential the house that explained the work, which is
  worth more than the house that did the work quietly.
- **Evidence.** `production-lifecycle-model.md` (the whole document), the discovery scripts
  in `docs/call-scripts/`, `product-spec-clearance-certified.md`.
- **Honesty constraint.** Every worked campaign is a teaching campaign with an invented
  brand, labelled as such, until a client approves a real one.

### Pillar 4 — The Revision Room
**The question it answers:** *How do I give feedback that gets me what I want in one round,
and what does a round actually cost?*

- **Purpose.** Turn the most contested part of every engagement, the notes, into a shared
  vocabulary between buyer and house.
- **Target audience.** Creative Directors (primary), agency producers, Brand Managers,
  Music Supervisors.
- **Topics.** Conform versus revision versus scope change. The round as a countable unit
  and when its clock starts. The feedback dictionary. Defend or comply. Fidelity
  calibration. Creative Lock and what it freezes. Consolidated notes. The hero element and
  how notes erode it. The round-three brand veto and how to prevent it at kickoff. Every
  note priced before a creator sees it. Timecoded notes against picture. What "more
  premium" can mean musically.
- **Why it builds authority.** This pillar makes the reader better at their own job
  tomorrow. Content that changes how a CD writes their next note is the definition of
  content people send to coworkers.
- **Evidence.** `production-lifecycle-model.md` Phase D, §5, §8, §10; ADR-0069, ADR-0094,
  ADR-0095, ADR-0096.
- **Honesty constraint.** Notes quoted as examples are invented or anonymised beyond
  recognition. A real client's note is never quoted without written approval.

### Pillar 5 — Campaign Delivery
**The question it answers:** *What should I actually receive, in what form, and how do I
know the delivery is complete?*

- **Purpose.** Publish the delivery standard the industry does not have: what a complete
  music delivery contains, to what spec, with what paperwork.
- **Target audience.** Agency producers, post producers and audio-post mixers, Brand
  content leads, Executive Producers.
- **Topics.** Delivery specs at kickoff. Stem splits cut to the post house. Loudness by
  medium. The version family. Naming and manifests. The delivery package and what rides

  11pm stem emergency and the escalation lane. Archive and re-use at renewal. The
  scope-against-the-brief checklist.
- **Why it builds authority.** A published, downloadable delivery spec that post houses
  adopt is the most referenced asset Chordential can create, and it is procurement-grade
  by nature. It makes "delivered" mean something specific and makes Chordential the name
  attached to the meaning.
- **Evidence.** `production-lifecycle-model.md` Phase F and H, `delivery-os-user-manual.md`,
  the Delivery OS agents (Rights, Revisions, Metadata, Approvals, Assets).
- **Honesty constraint.** The spec is a standard, not a boast. Where the industry's practice
  varies (stem conventions, loudness targets), say so and cite the standards bodies.

### Pillar 6 — The Producer's Desk
**The question it answers:** *How do the best producers run a music engagement so that
nothing surprises them?*

- **Purpose.** Write down the tacit operating craft of an Executive Producer, the part of

- **Target audience.** Executive Producers (primary), agency producers, Agency Owners,

- **Topics.** Ball-in-court state and its age. The two calendars and buffer burn-down. The

- **Why it builds authority.** This is the pillar that earns respect from peers, not just
  buyers. When EPs at other houses read it and recognise their own unwritten rules, the

- **Evidence.** `production-lifecycle-model.md` §2, §5, §6, §10; `client-workspace-principles.md`.
- **Honesty constraint.** Founder-run studio. Where a practice is ours by design rather than
  proven over a hundred projects, say which.

### Pillar 7 — The Studio as a System
**The question it answers:** *How should a creative-service business run so that it gets
better with every job instead of starting over?*

- **Purpose.** The founder's perspective and the transparent view of how Chordential runs,

  it lives here as transparency, never as promotion.
- **Target audience.** Studio owners and operators, Agency Owners who run a creative

- **Topics.** The three failures (latency, relationships in one head, a business that never

## 3. Stage 3 — Recurring series

Think like a publication: a series is a repeatable shape with a fixed structure, a fixed
cadence, a pillar it belongs to, and one thing the reader leaves smarter about. Eleven
series plus one reference library. Every published piece belongs to exactly one series.

Each series below carries its **shape** (the recurring structure every episode follows),
its **save mechanic** (the reason someone keeps it), and its **send mechanic** (the reason
someone forwards it). A piece with neither mechanic is not published.

### 3.1 Behind the Brief — *Pillar 3*
- **Shape.** One brief shape per episode (a holiday spot, a social sprint, a sonic logo, a
  three-minute brand film). Show the brief as an agency writes it, the translation into
  musician language, what the house had to assume because the brief did not say, and what
  it would have cost to state it. Invented brands throughout.
- **Cadence.** Monthly.
- **Save mechanic.** The translation table: what the brief said, what the house heard.
- **Send mechanic.** A CD sends it to the producer before the next music brief goes out.

### 3.2 What Actually Happens Next — *Pillar 3*
- **Shape.** "You just [approved a direction / sent notes / signed the licence]. Here is
  what happens in the next seventy-two hours, who does it, what it freezes, and what could
  go wrong." One gate per episode, in lifecycle order, then repeated with variations.
- **Cadence.** Fortnightly for the first cycle (nine gates), then monthly.
- **Save mechanic.** Each episode ends with the same one-screen gate card: what this
  approval means, what it does not, what to ask before you give it.
- **Send mechanic.** The producer sends the gate card to the client at the moment of the
  approval.

### 3.3 The Revision Room — *Pillar 4*
- **Shape.** One note, decoded. The note as a client would write it; the three ways a house
  could read it; the musical moves each reading implies; the question that would have
  settled it in one round. A second recurring variant: one conform-versus-revision case,
  adjudicated in public.
- **Cadence.** Fortnightly.
- **Save mechanic.** The growing public feedback dictionary: note → possible meanings →
  the clarifying question.
- **Send mechanic.** Producers send the relevant entry with their consolidated notes.

### 3.4 Anatomy of a Delivery — *Pillar 5*
- **Shape.** Open one delivery package for a teaching campaign, file by file: what each
  file is, who consumes it, what spec it was cut to, and what happens downstream if it is
  missing. Rotate the campaign shape; rotate the failure ("the missing split", "the
  loudness surprise", "the cue sheet nobody filed").
- **Cadence.** Monthly.
- **Save mechanic.** The delivery manifest template.
- **Send mechanic.** Producers send it to post; post sends it back to producers.

### 3.5 One Minute Producer — *Pillar 6* (short-form spine)
- **Shape.** One operating rule, stated in under a minute, with the reason and the failure
  it prevents. Ball-in-court. The silence ladder. Specs at kickoff. Never book a vocalist
  pre-lock. The free-extra ledger. This is the format that carries the other series into
  short-form channels; every long piece yields at least one.
- **Cadence.** Weekly.
- **Save mechanic.** Numbered, so the collection becomes a producer's rulebook.
- **Send mechanic.** Short enough to forward without a preamble.

### 3.6 Music Myths — *Pillars 1 and 2*
- **Shape.** One belief the market holds; the evidence; the correction; what to do instead.
  "Royalty-free means free of copyright." "Exclusivity is included." "The platform library
  covers our paid posts." "A quote should land on the budget." Cited every time.
- **Cadence.** Fortnightly.
- **Save mechanic.** The myth list, indexed, becomes the thing people check before they
  sign.
- **Send mechanic.** "This is the thing I was trying to explain."

### 3.7 Where the Money Goes — *Pillar 1*
- **Shape.** One fee lever per episode, with sources: term, territory, media, exclusivity,
  featured placement, the creative fee, agency overhead, library economics, music
  supervision. Each ends with the same worked table showing the lever moving a teaching
  campaign's number, priced through the real model so nothing is restated by hand.
- **Cadence.** Monthly.
- **Save mechanic.** The lever card and the cited multiplier.
- **Send mechanic.** Sent to whoever has to approve the budget.

### 3.8 The Paper Trail — *Pillar 2*
- **Shape.** One document per episode, explained clause by clause in plain English: the
  cue sheet, the work-for-hire agreement, the licence, the clearance certificate, the
  composer agreement, the service agreement, the W-9 and COI, the acceptance record.
  What it protects, who signs it, what happens without it, what a bad one looks like.
- **Cadence.** Monthly.
- **Save mechanic.** An annotated specimen of the document, invented parties, per episode.
- **Send mechanic.** Producers send it to business affairs; business affairs sends it to
  brand legal.

### 3.9 Campaign Breakdown — *Pillars 3, 4 and 5*
- **Shape.** A public campaign's music, analysed as a set of *decisions*: what the music is
  doing, what the direction choice implies, how the version family was probably handled,
  what the licence must have covered given where it ran. Written as outside analysis of
  public work, credited to the house that made it where known. The honesty rule is
  absolute here: never imply involvement, never speculate about budgets as fact, never
  disparage. Award-season and Super Bowl editions are the tentpoles.
- **Cadence.** Monthly, weekly during the Super Bowl and Cannes windows.
- **Save mechanic.** The decision map of a spot people have seen.
- **Send mechanic.** Timeliness. It exists while people are talking about the spot.

### 3.10 Inside ChordOS — *Pillar 7*
- **Shape.** One rule of the operating system and the reason it exists, told as a story of
  the failure it answers. The human on the decision is always visible. The software is the
  illustration; the rule is the content. Drawn from the architecture decisions, which are
  already written as reasons.
- **Cadence.** Monthly.
- **Save mechanic.** The rule, stated once, quotable.
- **Send mechanic.** Operators at other studios recognise their own problem.

### 3.11 The Operator's Ledger — *Pillar 7* (founder perspective)
- **Shape.** Quarterly letter from the founder: what the studio learned, what it changed,
  what it got wrong, dated and specific. Numbers live or labelled demo. This is the one
  series where opinion is expected, and the one where the honesty rule is most on display.
- **Cadence.** Quarterly.
- **Save mechanic.** Candour compounds. The letters become a public record of a studio
  being built.
- **Send mechanic.** Peers and press.

### 3.12 Ask the Producer — *All pillars* (community)
- **Shape.** Real questions from readers, answered in full, anonymised. The questions feed
  the discovery keyword lists and the prep sheet, so the content program teaches the
  operating system what buyers actually ask.
- **Cadence.** Monthly.
- **Save mechanic.** The answer to the exact question someone had.
- **Send mechanic.** "Someone asked your question."

### 3.13 The Reference Library — *All pillars* (evergreen, the durable core)
Not a series but the destination the series build toward. Long-form reference pieces
that are maintained, dated and versioned, living on chordential.com and nowhere else
first. These are the assets the "would the industry miss it" test is really about.

| Reference asset | Pillar | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| The Music Licence Decoder (term, media, territory, exclusivity, with cited multipliers) | 1, 2 | Where the Money Goes, Music Myths |
| The Kickoff Checklist (what to settle before a note is written, specs included) | 3, 6 | What Actually Happens Next, One Minute Producer |
| The Delivery Standard (the open spec: stems, loudness, naming, manifest) | 5 | Anatomy of a Delivery |
| The Cue Sheet Primer for Advertisers | 2 | The Paper Trail |
| The Feedback Dictionary (public, growing) | 4 | The Revision Room |
| The Lifecycle Map (nine phases, gates, who decides what) | 3, 6 | What Actually Happens Next, Behind the Brief |
| The Stakeholder Census (who controls what on a campaign) | 6 | The Producer's Desk |
| What a Clearance Certificate Contains | 2 | The Paper Trail |
| The Glossary of Commercial Music (conform, round, Creative Lock, chain of title, needle drop, buyout, MFN, cue sheet, stem, lift…) | All | Every series |
| Questions to Ask Any Music House (including us) | 1, 2, 5 | Music Myths, The Paper Trail |

**Library rule.** A reference asset is published only when it can be maintained. Each
carries a "last reviewed" date and an owner. An asset nobody will keep current is not
published, because a stale reference is the honesty rule broken slowly.

## 4. Stage 4 — Mapping content to the buyer journey

Six stages. For each: the question in the reader's head, what content answers it, which
series carry it, and the signal that tells us it worked. The journey is not linear; a
reader can enter at Trust by being sent a checklist and never see Awareness. That is the
design: the sendable assets are the entry points.

| Stage | The reader's question | What belongs here | Series | Signal it worked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | *"I didn't know that was a thing."* | Short, surprising, cited corrections and rules. Timely analysis of work people have seen. | Music Myths · One Minute Producer · Campaign Breakdown | Saves and forwards from people who have never visited the site. |
| **Education** | *"How does this actually work?"* | The serialised lifecycle, the money levers, the documents. Longer, structured, indexed. | What Actually Happens Next · Where the Money Goes · The Paper Trail · Behind the Brief | Return visits to the same series; time on the reference library; newsletter subscriptions. |
| **Trust** | *"These people know what they're doing, and they'll tell me the truth."* | Transparency about method and mistakes. The public dictionary. The open delivery standard. Content that says when you don't need us. | The Revision Room · Anatomy of a Delivery · Inside ChordOS · The Operator's Ledger · Ask the Producer | People cite Chordential to each other. Inbound that references a specific piece. Peers at other houses engaging. |
| **Consideration** | *"What would working with them actually be like?"* | The engagement walked week by week, from the client's side. The certificate explained. The round policy stated. A proposal's itemised derivation, explained. The worked pricing example. | What Actually Happens Next (client-side edition) · The Paper Trail (certificate, licence) · Where the Money Goes (the worked example) · the `/pricing` page | Discovery calls that arrive already knowing the vocabulary. Fewer "why two fees" objections. |
| **Decision** | *"Why them and not the cheaper option?"* | The honest comparison: house versus freelancer versus library versus AI, what each fails to deliver and where each is fine. A client-approved case study, when one exists. The questions to ask any music house. | Music Myths (the comparison editions) · Questions to Ask Any Music House · the first real case study, slotted into Anatomy of a Delivery | Proposal acceptance without a price-only negotiation. The buyer repeating our distinctions back to us. |
| **Referral** | *"Who should I send this to?"* | The sendable artifacts, designed to be forwarded to the person who needs them: the checklist to the producer, the decoder to business affairs, the delivery spec to post, the cue sheet primer to legal. | The Reference Library · the gate cards · the lever cards · the manifest template | Referrals that name the artifact. Post houses and BA teams adopting the spec. Second-degree readers. |

**Two rules of the map.**

1. **Nothing at Decision is written to close.** The honesty rule means the comparison
   content says when a library track is the right answer. That is what makes the rest
   believable.
2. **Referral content is engineered, not hoped for.** Every reference asset names the role
   it should be forwarded to, on the asset itself. The buyer is rarely the reader who
   needs it most; business affairs, post and legal are the hidden audiences, and each has
   a piece addressed to them.

**How the journey feeds the operating system.** Questions from Ask the Producer and the
objections surfaced in Consideration are routed to the discovery prep sheet and keyword
lists, so what buyers ask in public sharpens what the machine proposes in private. The
content program is a sensor, not only a speaker.

## 5. Stage 5 — The publishing mix

Value over promotion, with numbers so it can be audited. The mix is measured quarterly
against what actually shipped, by count of pieces.

| Kind | Share | What it means here | Ceiling or floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Educational** | 35% | Teaching a mechanism: how a licence works, what a round costs, what a cue sheet does. Cited. | Floor: never below 30%. |
| **Process** | 15% | Step-by-step operating craft: checklists, gate cards, specs, the kickoff sequence. | — |
| **Behind the scenes** | 10% | The taste gate, the two calendars, the writing room, told through teaching campaigns. | Invented brands only until approved. |
| **Industry analysis** | 10% | Campaign Breakdowns, the honest market map, rulings on AI and copyright as they land. | Analysis of public work, attributed; never involvement implied. |
| **Opinion** | 8% | Positions taken with reasons: conform is not a revision; the budget is not the price; volume is not a metric. | Every opinion cites the evidence it rests on. |
| **Founder perspective** | 7% | The Operator's Ledger and its offcuts. Candour, dated. | — |
| **Case studies** | 5% | Teaching campaigns now; real, client-approved cases when they exist. The share grows only as real ones accrue. | Never an invented success presented as real. |
| **Product** | 5% | Inside ChordOS. A rule illustrated by the system, with the human on the decision visible. | Ceiling: never above 10%, even in a launch quarter. |
| **Community** | 5% | Ask the Producer, hosted composer and post-house contributions, credited. | — |

**Three mix rules.**

- **Product is a form of transparency or it is cut.** A product piece must leave a reader
  smarter about the domain even if they never use the product. If the rule is removed and
  only the feature remains, it fails.
- **Every piece is cited or it is opinion, and it is labelled which.** Educational,
  process and analysis pieces carry sources. Opinion is marked as opinion.
- **Every piece ships with its sendable.** A gate card, a lever card, a specimen document,
  a checklist line. The sendable is designed first and the piece is written toward it.

**Formats and channels, as a system, not as posts.**

- **The home is the reference library on chordential.com.** Everything is published there
  first and lives there permanently at a URL that does not change. Rented channels
  distribute; they never hold the only copy.
- **Long-form** (the reference pieces and serialised episodes) is the primary unit of value.
- **Short-form** (One Minute Producer and the cuts from every long piece) is the primary
  unit of distribution.
- **A monthly letter** carries the month's theme, the new reference assets and the founder's
  note to the people who have asked to hear from us. It is the relationship channel.
- **Sound.** We are a music company. Any audio in our content is human-made and credited to
  the person who made it. No AI-generated audio, anywhere, ever, including background beds.
- **Visual grammar.** Diagrams of mechanisms (the two tracks, the gate ladder, the version
  chain) over decoration. Monospace for evidence and numbers, in the spirit of the
  Constitution's "instrumentation, not marketing."

## 7. The editorial system (how future content creators execute this)

### 7.1 The piece brief

No piece is written without a one-page brief that answers, in this order:

1. **Pillar and series.** Exactly one of each.
2. **Journey stage.** Which question in the reader's head it answers.
3. **The smarter-after statement.** One sentence: *after reading this, a [role] will know
   [mechanism] and will [do something differently].* If it cannot be written, the piece
   is not worth making.
4. **The sendable.** What the reader forwards, and to whom.
5. **Sources.** The primary documents, dated. For anything priced: the rate card or
   research page it rests on.
6. **Honesty check.** Invented brands named. No real client implied. Any capability
   mentioned exists today. Numbers live, cited or labelled demo. Legal content marked as
   educational.
7. **Owner and review date** if it enters the reference library.

### 7.2 Editorial standards (binding)

- **Evidence first.** A claim without a source is an opinion and is labelled one.
- **Teaching campaigns only** until a client-approved case exists: AURORA, Vance Athletic,
  Lumen Health, Northwind Coffee, and new invented brands added to a register so none
  collides with a real trademark.
- **Never disparage a named competitor.** Analyse mechanisms, not companies. The
  differentiation table in the positioning brief is for sales conversations, not for
  publication.
- **Say when you don't need us.** A library track is sometimes the right answer. Content
  that admits it is the content that gets believed.
- **No AI-generated audio, no AI-generated imagery presented as photography, and no
  AI-written piece published without a named human editor who stands behind every claim.**
  The machine may draft; a person disposes.
- **Plain language, producer-voiced.** The same voice as the workspace terms: premium,
  specific, never boilerplate. Expand every acronym the first time.
- **Dated and versioned.** Reference assets carry a last-reviewed date. A stale reference
  is retired, not left up.
- **Credit the craft.** Every composer, mixer, post house or producer whose work or
  contribution appears is named and linked, with permission.

### 7.3 Roles

| Role | Owns |
|---|---|
| **Editor** (the Content Strategist seat on the Marketing Council) | The calendar, the piece briefs, the standards, the quarterly audit, the reference library's review dates. |
| **Writer-producer** | The episodes and the cuts. Works from the brief and the primary sources. |
| **Designer** | The diagrams of mechanisms, the sendable cards, the specimen documents. |
| **The founder** | The Operator's Ledger, Inside ChordOS, and final say on any honesty-rule question. |
| **Domain reviewers** (rotating: a producer, a post mixer, a business-affairs contact, a composer) | Read before publication. Credited. Their objections are documented, in the tradition of the board simulations. |

### 7.4 What we do not publish

- Fabricated case studies, testimonials, credits or metrics.
- Real client names, notes, budgets or documents without written approval.
- Quotes presented as prices. Bands and levers, with sources.
- Predictions about rulings, lawsuits or platform policy.
- Content about the software that is not first content about the domain.
- Engagement bait, hot takes on people, trend-chasing, or anything whose only purpose is
  reach. Volume is never a success metric.
- Anything the studio cannot stand behind on a discovery call the next morning.

### 7.5 Measurement

Followers are not measured. The program is judged on whether people keep it, forward it
and cite it, and on whether the studio's conversations get shorter because the buyer
already knows the vocabulary.

| Measure | What it tells us | Where it is read |
|---|---|---|
| **Saves and forwards** per piece | Whether it was worth keeping. | Channel analytics, newsletter forwards. |
| **Reference-library returns** | Whether a piece became a reference. | Site analytics on the library URLs. |
| **Citations** (a producer, a post house, a BA team referencing a piece or adopting a spec) | Whether the industry is using it. | Logged by hand in the relationship record; it is Relationship Intelligence. |
| **Inbound that names a piece** | Whether trust arrived before the call. | The discovery prep sheet asks "how did you find us"; the answer is logged. |
| **Vocabulary on discovery calls** | Whether buyers arrive already knowing conform versus revision, the two fees, the certificate. | The call copilot's transcript scoring. |
| **The publishing mix, as shipped** | Whether we kept to Stage 5. | Quarterly count against the table. |
| **The audit** | The one question. | Section 8, quarterly. |

## 8. The quarterly audit: "would the industry miss it?"

Every quarter, the Editor and the founder answer the graded question by answering these
ten, in writing, with evidence, and file the answers in `docs/` beside this strategy.

1. Which reference asset did someone outside Chordential **use** this quarter, and how do
   we know?
2. Which piece was **forwarded to a role we did not write it for** (legal, post,
   procurement), and by whom?
3. Did any **peer at another house** engage with a Producer's Desk or Inside ChordOS piece
   as if it were about their own work?
4. Did a buyer arrive at a discovery call **already using our vocabulary**? Which words?
5. Did we publish anything we would have to **walk back** on a call? If so, what changed
   in the standards?
6. Did every reference asset get **reviewed by its date**, and was anything retired?
7. What did the audience **ask** (Ask the Producer, objections, search terms) that we have
   no piece for, and which pillar does it belong to?
8. What is the **most-saved piece** of the quarter, and what does it have that the
   least-saved piece lacks?
9. Did the **publishing mix** hold, and was product under its ceiling?
10. If the site went dark tomorrow, **name the three assets** someone would email us asking
    for. If we cannot name three, the next quarter's campaign is chosen to change that.

The answer to the graded question is *yes* only when questions 1, 2 and 10 have specific,
evidenced answers three quarters running. Until then, the program keeps refining, and
this document is amended, dated, with the reason.
